## Description

Felix is an unassuming, well-dressed man who operates in the city's supernatural underground - the kind of person you'd walk past on the street without a second glance. That's exactly how he prefers it. While werewolf packs tear each other apart and vampire lords wage shadow wars, Felix sits in quiet rooms and speaks in calm tones, and somehow, impossibly, people do what he tells them to do.

He doesn't carry weapons. He doesn't throw punches. He doesn't need to. His voice carries a weight that can break a man's mind, a subtle supernatural resonance that makes his suggestions feel like inevitabilities. Those who know him understand: Felix doesn't do violence, but he can arrange fates far worse. He is the information broker, the dealmaker, the man who knows everyone's secrets and everyone's debts - including his own.

And that's the problem. Felix's network of contacts, informants, and questionable allies is vast, but it's built on a foundation of favors owed and debts unpaid. Every time he calls in help, the price goes up. Every secret he learns, he pays for in obligations. In the supernatural underworld, loyalty is currency - and Felix's credit is running dangerously thin.

## Aspects

### High Concept
**I Can Be *Very* Persuasive**

*Felix's voice carries supernatural weight when he speaks calmly and deliberately. Invokable for social manipulation, making deals, giving commands, and persuading people to see things his way. Compellable when he cannot resort to violence even when it would be easier, when his manipulation creates resentment or enemies, or when someone realizes they've been played and seeks revenge.*

### Trouble
**Indebted to a Network of Questionable Loyalty**

*Felix's power comes from his contacts, but every favor has a price, and the bill is coming due. Invokable when calling in favors from shady contacts, leveraging existing debts, or using his network to accomplish the impossible. Compellable when the network demands payment at the worst time, when contacts betray him to save themselves, when debts conflict with his goals, or when his allies' questionable loyalty puts him in danger.*

### Phase One: Your Methodology
**My Network is My Net Worth**

*Felix has spent years cultivating relationships across the supernatural underground - from street-level informants to power brokers. Invokable when he needs a specific contact, resource, or introduction, or to have "a guy" for any situation. Compellable when maintaining the network costs him time, money, or creates new obligations, or when his contacts have agendas that conflict with his own.*

### Phase Two: Your Asset
**Eyes and Ears on Every Corner**

*Felix's surveillance network is extensive - informants, cameras, strategically placed allies, and a supernatural knack for knowing things he shouldn't. Invokable for Notice checks, to have already spotted something important, to know about events before they become public, or to have an escape route prepared. Compellable when his surveillance network is compromised, when he learns too much to act on all of it, or when people realize they're being watched and take action against him.*

### Phase Three: Your Reputation
**A Fate Worse Than Violence**

*In the supernatural underground, Felix is known for elegant, terrible revenge. He doesn't kill - he destroys reputations, exposes secrets, turns allies into enemies, and arranges "accidents" that leave victims wishing for a clean death. Invokable to intimidate without physical threats, to have his reputation precede him, or to make threats that people believe. Compellable when his reputation makes people refuse to deal with him, when enemies strike preemptively out of fear, or when someone calls his bluff and forces him to follow through.*

### Skill Descriptions

- **Rapport (+4)**: Felix's primary weapon - building trust, making deals, charming marks, and (via The Voice of Command) giving orders that feel like suggestions. This is his most dangerous skill.
- **Contacts (+3)**: The network Felix has built over years - street informants, supernatural power brokers, corrupt officials, and everyone in between. He always knows someone who knows someone.
- **Empathy (+3)**: Reading people like open books - their desires, fears, lies, and hidden motives. Felix uses this defensively to anticipate social attacks and offensively to find pressure points.
- **Provoke (+2)**: Making threats, creating fear, and breaking people psychologically. Felix rarely raises his voice, but when he describes what he'll do to someone's reputation, they believe him.
- **Will (+2)**: Mental fortitude and resistance to supernatural influence. Felix needs this to maintain composure and resist the very manipulation tactics he uses on others.
- **Investigate (+2)**: Researching targets, following paper trails, connecting dots, and building intelligence files on people and organizations.
- **Deceive (+1)**: Lying convincingly, creating cover stories, and misdirection. Felix prefers truth with strategic omissions, but he can lie when necessary.
- **Notice (+1)**: Spotting details, surveillance, and threats. Supplemented heavily by his "Eyes and Ears on Every Corner" aspect.
- **Resources (+1)**: Access to money and material goods. Felix isn't wealthy, but he can usually scrape together what he needs or borrow it.
- **Burglary (+1)**: Breaking and entering, bypassing security, and finding unconventional exits - useful for escaping when deals go bad.

## Stunts

### 1. The Voice of Command
**Because you are "Very Persuasive"**, when you speak to someone calmly and they can hear your voice, you can use **Rapport** instead of Provoke to intimidate or give orders.

*This is your signature ability - it turns your highest skill (+4) into a terrifying intimidation tool. You can make threats, give commands, and break people's will without ever raising your voice. The Voice of Command is what makes Felix truly dangerous.*

---

### 2. Network Saturation
**Because you have "Eyes and Ears on Every Corner"**, once per session you can spend a fate point to automatically discover a secret about a person or location without having to make a roll.

*This is your information trump card - once per session, you simply KNOW something crucial. Use it strategically on the most dangerous enemies or the most critical mysteries. No roll, no failure - your network delivers.*

---

### 3. Cold Reader
**Because you can see right through people**, you get **+2 to Empathy** when trying to discover a person's Aspects (like their Trouble or hidden motivations) if you have spoken with them for at least a minute.

*Combined with your Empathy +3, this gives you +5 to discover what makes people tick. Talk to someone briefly, read their Aspects, then exploit them. This makes you exceptional at psychological warfare and social manipulation.*

## Backstory Summary

### The Man Who Knows Too Much
Felix started as a street-level information broker - nothing supernatural, just a guy with good ears and better connections. He'd sell tips to the highest bidder, connect people who needed connecting, and stay carefully neutral in gang wars and territorial disputes. It was profitable and relatively safe, as long as he remembered the cardinal rule: never get personally involved.

Then one night, a desperate vampire offered him a deal: information about a rival coven in exchange for a favor "to be called in later." Felix took it. The information was gold. The favor came due six months later and cost him three more favors to fulfill. Those three favors cascaded into a dozen more. Within a year, Felix realized he'd been played - not by the vampire, but by the entire supernatural economy. Everyone owed everyone else, and the debts never cleared, they just accumulated.

### The Voice
Somewhere in those desperate months of juggling debts and favors, Felix discovered he could make people listen. Not with threats or money, but with his voice itself. A calm suggestion that felt like an inevitability. An order that seemed reasonable even when it wasn't. He doesn't know if it's a supernatural gift he always had, a side effect of the deals he made, or something the city gave him for surviving its underworld. He just knows that when he speaks, people obey.

The Voice made him valuable. Dangerous. Feared. Felix used it to climb the ladder, consolidating debts, expanding his network, and building a reputation: "Felix doesn't do violence." It became his brand, his protection, and his curse. Because now, even when violence would solve his problems cleanly, his reputation won't let him use it. He's trapped in his own persona.

### The Tightening Noose
Felix's network is vast, but the foundation is crumbling. Too many favors owed, too many contacts with their own agendas, too many debts coming due simultaneously. The supernatural underground is heating up - vampire covens at war, werewolf packs fragmenting, fae courts making moves - and everyone wants Felix's help. Everyone wants him to choose a side. Everyone wants payment.

And the terrifying truth Felix won't admit: he's not sure his network is loyal anymore. They fear him, yes. They need him, maybe. But loyalty? That's a currency he's been spending without earning back. Sooner or later, when he calls for help, no one will answer. And then the man who built everything on connections will be utterly, completely alone.

## Notes & Reminders

- **Avoid Physical Combat**: Only 2 Physical Stress boxes and no combat skills - Felix must talk, escape, or concede
- **The Voice is Your Weapon**: Rapport +4 via Voice of Command lets you intimidate at your highest skill
- **Network Saturation is Once Per Session**: Use strategically on the most critical mystery or dangerous enemy
- **Cold Reader Setup**: Talk to targets for 1 minute, then roll Empathy +5 to discover their Aspects
- **Mental Stress = 3 boxes**: You can absorb some social attacks, but you're not a tank
- **Play Your Trouble**: Accept compels on your network's questionable loyalty to generate Fate Points when you need them
- **Narrative Positioning**: Always establish escape routes before entering dangerous scenes
- **Concede Early**: When physically threatened, concede to define the outcome (escape but owe a favor, lose the MacGuffin, etc.)
- **Describe The Voice**: Make it cinematic - whispers that echo, calm words that chill, suggestions that feel like compulsions

## Play Styles

Felix can be played in multiple ways depending on what aspects of his concept you emphasize. Here are six distinct approaches:

### Style 1: The Puppet Master (Manipulation & Control)

**Core Approach**: Never get your hands dirty. Pull strings from the shadows.

**Tactics**:
- Use The Voice of Command to give orders and redirect threats toward each other
- Invoke "My Network is My Net Worth" to have the right contact for any situation
- Cold Reader to discover enemy Aspects, then use Create Advantage to exploit them
- Maintain distance from physical conflict; position yourself as indispensable

**Key Moves**:
- **Triangulation**: Use Rapport/Contacts to set two enemies against each other
- **Information Leverage**: Gather secrets with Network Saturation, then threaten to expose them
- **Proxy Violence**: Have others do your fighting while you coordinate

**Fate Point Economy**:
- **Spend on**: Invoking "My Network is My Net Worth" and "Eyes and Ears on Every Corner"
- **Earn from**: Accepting compels when your network's loyalty wavers at the worst time

**Strengths**: Maximum control, keeps you safe from physical danger, leverages your highest skills
**Weaknesses**: Dependent on network reliability, can backfire if contacts betray you, requires setup time

---

### Style 2: The Velvet Glove (Charm & Persuasion)

**Core Approach**: Win them over before they even realize they've lost.

**Tactics**:
- Lead with Rapport (+4) in every social encounter to build trust first
- Use Cold Reader to identify what people want, then offer it (with strings attached)
- Invoke "I Can Be *Very* Persuasive" when making deals or negotiating
- Frame every interaction as mutually beneficial, even when it isn't

**Key Moves**:
- **The Soft Sell**: Use Empathy to discover Aspects, then offer solutions that create obligations
- **Charm Offensive**: Stack bonuses with Rapport + Voice of Command + Aspect invokes for overwhelming social attacks
- **Guilt Trips**: Use Empathy and Rapport together to make people feel they owe you

**Fate Point Economy**:
- **Spend on**: Invoking your High Concept "I Can Be *Very* Persuasive" to close deals
- **Earn from**: Having your charm backfire when people realize you've manipulated them

**Strengths**: Plays to your +4 Rapport, creates long-term allies (with debts), feels smooth and natural
**Weaknesses**: Enemies eventually wise up, relies on social skills, people may resent manipulation

---

### Style 3: The Information Broker (Investigation & Leverage)

**Core Approach**: Knowledge is power. Everyone has secrets. Know them all.

**Tactics**:
- Use Network Saturation strategically on the most dangerous or mysterious NPCs
- Investigate (+2) and Contacts (+3) in tandem to build comprehensive intelligence files
- Create Advantages constantly: "Knows Their Weakness," "Has Blackmail Material," "Predicted This Move"
- Sell information to multiple parties, playing both sides

**Key Moves**:
- **Pre-Scene Prep**: Before confrontations, use Contacts to gather intel and Create Advantages
- **The Reveal**: At the perfect moment, use Cold Reader or invoke "Eyes and Ears on Every Corner" to drop a bombshell that changes the power dynamic
- **Information Hostage**: Make yourself too valuable to kill by ensuring secrets die with you

**Fate Point Economy**:
- **Spend on**: Network Saturation (once per session) and invoking "Eyes and Ears on Every Corner"
- **Earn from**: Your network feeding you bad intel or demanding payment for information

**Strengths**: Network Saturation guarantees crucial information, creates narrative control, highly versatile
**Weaknesses**: Once-per-session limit on signature ability, requires investigative hooks from GM, can create too many enemies

---

### Style 4: The Intimidator (Terror Without Violence)

**Core Approach**: Violence is crude. Terror is elegant.

**Tactics**:
- Invoke "A Fate Worse Than Violence" when making threats
- Use Voice of Command to give orders with the weight of supernatural consequence
- Provoke (+2) backed by Rapport (+4) creates calm, terrifying threats
- Describe in detail what you'll do to someone's reputation, family, or sanity

**Key Moves**:
- **The Quiet Threat**: Use Rapport (via Voice of Command) to intimidate without raising your voice
- **Reputation Damage**: Threaten to destroy someone's name using your Contacts network
- **Psychological Warfare**: Use Empathy to find what someone fears most, then invoke "A Fate Worse Than Violence" to weaponize it

**Fate Point Economy**:
- **Spend on**: Invoking "A Fate Worse Than Violence" to make threats stick
- **Earn from**: When your reputation makes people act against you preemptively

**Strengths**: Incredibly effective at creating fear, uses your best skills for intimidation, memorable scenes
**Weaknesses**: Can create permanent enemies, relies on reputation (can be called as bluff), morally dark

---

### Style 5: The Escape Artist (Survival & Evasion)

**Core Approach**: If you can talk your way in, you can talk your way out.

**Tactics**:
- Deceive (+1) supported by high Empathy (+3) to craft believable lies
- Invoke "Eyes and Ears on Every Corner" to always have an escape route prepared
- Notice (+1) and Burglary (+1) to spot and use unconventional exits
- Use Rapport to make enemies hesitate or second-guess themselves

**Key Moves**:
- **The Misdirection**: Use Create Advantage with Deceive or Rapport to create "Distracted," "Chasing Ghosts," or "Looking the Wrong Way"
- **The Network Escape**: Invoke "My Network is My Net Worth" to have a safehouse or extraction ready
- **The Bluff**: Use Voice of Command to order someone to stand down or let you pass, even when you have no real leverage

**Fate Point Economy**:
- **Spend on**: Invoking aspects to escape or create escape-related Advantages
- **Earn from**: Situations where your network's disloyalty cuts off your escape

**Strengths**: Hard to pin down, great for survival-focused play, creates exciting chase scenes
**Weaknesses**: Reactive rather than proactive, lower escape-focused skills (+1), relies heavily on Fate Points

---

### Style 6: The Social Tank (Mental Resilience)

**Core Approach**: You can't break what you can't touch, and you can't touch me.

**Tactics**:
- Leverage Will (+2) and Mental Stress [3] to absorb social and mental attacks
- Use Cold Reader defensively to anticipate social attacks and defend against them
- Invoke "I Can Be *Very* Persuasive" not just to attack, but to defend with counter-arguments
- Position yourself as untouchable through reputation and connections

**Key Moves**:
- **The Unmoved Mover**: Defend against Provoke/Intimidate with Rapport or Will, staying calm
- **Counter-Manipulation**: When someone tries to manipulate you, use Empathy to turn it back on them
- **Network Shield**: Invoke "My Network is My Net Worth" to have others take the fall or absorb consequences

**Fate Point Economy**:
- **Spend on**: Defending with Aspect invokes and soaking mental consequences
- **Earn from**: Your network creating problems that stress your mental resilience

**Strengths**: Difficult to break mentally, good for political intrigue, uses defensive skills effectively
**Weaknesses**: Only 3 Mental Stress boxes (not a true tank), doesn't solve problems directly, can be expensive

### Universal Tactics (All Styles)

**Aspect Invocation Priority**:
1. Most efficient: Invoke situation Aspects created by you or allies (free if you created them)
2. High impact: Invoke "I Can Be *Very* Persuasive" on Rapport rolls (thematic + effective at +4)
3. Emergency: Invoke multiple Aspects on critical rolls (costs add up but guarantees success)

**Compel Management**:
- Accept compels that generate interesting scenes—fate points are worth the complications
- Refuse compels that feel wrong—you can spend a fate point to refuse
- Suggest your own compels to earn GM trust: "Should my network betray me here?"

**Creating Advantages**:
Use your high skills to create stackable Aspects, then unleash 2-3 free invokes on one massive roll:
- **Rapport +4**: "They Trust Me," "Feeling Guilty," "Charmed and Obligated"
- **Contacts +3**: "I Know A Guy," "Called In Favors," "Network in Position"
- **Empathy +3**: "I Know Their Weakness," "Read Their True Motive," "Spotted the Lie"
- **Investigate +2**: "Found the Secret," "Paper Trail Exposed," "Connected the Dots"

**Stress Management**:
- **Physical Stress**: Only 2 boxes—AVOID physical conflict at all costs
- **Mental Stress**: 3 boxes—decent but not invincible, pick social battles carefully
- **Use Consequences narratively**: "Owes the Vampire Court (Mild)" or "Network Turning on Me (Moderate)" create story hooks

**Voice of Command Optimization**:
Since this lets you use Rapport (+4) for intimidation instead of Provoke (+2), always describe it as calm, controlled, and terrifying. The mechanical benefit is huge (+2 skill difference), but the narrative is what makes it memorable.

**Network Saturation Strategy**:
You only get this once per session—use it on:
- The most dangerous antagonist (learn their weakness/secret)
- The biggest mystery (what's really going on?)
- Emergency escapes (who can help me right now?)
Don't waste it on minor NPCs or information you could get with Contacts rolls.

**Hybrid Approaches**:
- **The Complete Operator** (Puppet Master + Information Broker): Gather intelligence, then use it to manipulate all sides. Maximum control.
- **The Feared Diplomat** (Velvet Glove + Intimidator): Charm first, threaten second. The carrot and the stick.
- **The Survivor** (Escape Artist + Social Tank): Impossible to pin down, impossible to break. Built for long campaigns.

**Recommended Starting Style**: Try Style 2 (The Velvet Glove) or Style 3 (The Information Broker) for your first session—both leverage your highest skills (Rapport +4, Contacts/Empathy +3), feel immediately powerful, and are mechanically straightforward. As you get comfortable, blend styles based on the situation.

**Most Important**: Fate Core rewards creative Aspect use. Felix's Aspects are double-edged by design—the most fun play style is the one that engages your Aspects aggressively, both invoking AND accepting compels. Felix is built for social intrigue and moral complexity. Lean into it. Remember: violence is for people without imagination, and Felix has plenty of imagination.
