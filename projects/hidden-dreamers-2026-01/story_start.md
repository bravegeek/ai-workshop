# Hadal Recovery: Scene 1 Draft

## The Threshold

**I. Proctor IO (The Vector)**
Aethelgard Tower. 03:00 AM. 
I check the timestamp on the security feed. Two minutes late.

The lobby is quiet, but it’s the wrong kind of quiet. The air filtration system is humming at a slightly higher pitch than yesterday—a bearing is loose in fan unit 4. I make a note to fire the maintenance chief if we survive this.

The doors slide open and Thorne walks in. He looks like a walking demolition site—leather trench coat, exposed ink, the smell of ozone and old sweat. He’s intentionally projecting "threat" to mask his anxiety. It’s working on the receptionist, but not on us.

"You're late," I say. My voice is steady. Inside, Oryn is running probability models on the mission success rate. The numbers are bad. I tell him to shut up.

**II. Elara Vance (The Chorus)**
The lights in here are too bright. They buzz. I can feel the vibration in my teeth.

I stay near the pillar, trying to keep my breathing shallow. The lobby is empty, but it doesn't feel empty. It feels crowded. The night shift security guard is thinking about his divorce again—a heavy, grey fog of regret that's drifting across the room. I pull my coat tighter. Being this close to IO is like standing next to a server rack; she gives off a low, constant hum of calculation that makes my head ache.

Thorne is worse. He walks in and the air pressure drops. He feels... heavy. Dense. Like a stone dropped in a pond.

"The Static Ceiling is low tonight," I say, trying to block out the guard's sadness. "Everyone is dreaming about falling."

**III. Kaelen Thorne (The Tethered)**
The elevator doors look like a tomb.

I step into the light and feel the familiar itch on my ribs. My skin knows where we're going before my brain does. The tattoos are reacting to the proximity of the Needle—a faint, burning sensation, like a sunburn under the leather.

The Proctor is watching me. She thinks she's hidden her fear behind that crisp suit and the corneal implants, but I can see the tension in her jaw. She's not a machine. She's just a woman holding her breath.

"Try not to bleed on the upholstery," she says.

I don't answer. I just check the seals on my coat and wait for the gravity to shift.

## The Descent

**I. Proctor IO (The Vector)**
The floor drops. 

The Executive Elevator isn't a cable-hoist; it’s a magnetic sled. We hit 10 m/s in three seconds. My stomach lurches—a physical protest Mira echoes in my head—but I suppress it. On the digital overlay, the "Metropolis Grid" is replaced by the "Needle Schematics."

Floor -10: The Shallows.

Outside the reinforced glass, the darkness of the Psychosphere rushes past. It’s not a void. It’s a medium—a thick, indigo fluid made of twenty million people’s subconscious noise. On my HUD, it looks like a swarm of bioluminescent data. To the others, I imagine it looks like hell.

"Prepare for the Threshold," I say. "Pressure will stabilize at Floor -50."

**II. Elara Vance (The Chorus)**
The elevator doesn't just fall; it *unravels*.

As soon as we pass the basement, the world outside the glass turns into a storm of neon static. It’s the city’s dreams, sheared off by the Needle’s psychic shielding. I press my forehead against the cool glass and close my eyes, but it doesn't help. I can hear the hum of the Metropolis above us—a billion unsaid words, a choir of anxieties, a low-frequency moan of collective sleep.

Then, we hit the Shallows. The noise changes. It gets deeper, slower. The chaotic sparks of the city turn into large, slow-moving shapes. 

I gasp. A massive, translucent whale-thing—made entirely of a child’s memory of the ocean—drifts past the elevator. It’s beautiful and heartbreakingly fragile.

"There's a pod in the Shallows," I whisper. "They're... they're searching for something."

**III. Kaelen Thorne (The Tethered)**
The drop hits me in the chest. 

My tattoos aren't itching anymore; they’re screaming. The Serpent Rune on my ribs starts to glow a dull, angry orange, shining through my shirt. I can feel the weight of the water—the *real* water—pressing against the glass. 

I look past the Proctor’s digital projections. I see the whale-thing Elara is talking about, but I don't see beauty. I see a scavenger. It’s a Shallows-Grave, a cluster of archetypes feeding on the city’s emotional runoff. 

I look down. The elevator shaft is a long, black throat. And we are going straight to the stomach.

"Ignore the fish, Elara," I growl, tightening the straps on my gloves. "They aren't the problem. Watch the floor. The Needle is vibrating. We’re passing through a pocket of The Static."
