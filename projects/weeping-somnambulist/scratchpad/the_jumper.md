## I. What It Is

A sentient psychic entity that inhabits conscious hosts sequentially, accumulating continuous identity and experience across each. Not a parasite in the ecological sense — that category implies passivity and no selfhood. The Jumper has both.

**Inspiration:** Azazel from *Fallen* — the horror of one continuous self that persists while bodies change. Adapted: transfer mechanism is psychic, not physical touch.

**Taxonomy problem:** It doesn't fit any existing ecological category cleanly.
- Not a Parasite (those are passive, non-sentient, feed on the act of remembering)
- Not a Symbiont (gives nothing back)
- Not part of the Immune Response
- Something distinct — a category of one, or nearly one

*Open: does it have a name in the world's own vocabulary? The crew may have no word for it yet.*

---

## II. The Consent Mechanic

The Jumper cannot force entry. It requires consent — genuine consent, not coerced compliance. The host opens the door from the inside.

This is the central horror: **you said yes. And you meant it.**

### The Emotional Lockpick

It has been inside enough people across enough centuries that it understands human psychological architecture with preternatural depth — not from reading minds, but from pattern recognition accumulated across hundreds of hosts. It knows the shape of grief, vanity, hunger for power, altruism, loneliness, pride. It has lived all of them from the inside.

It doesn't specialize in one weakness. It finds *your* specific lever — whatever it is that you organize yourself around — and presents itself in exactly the register that speaks to it:

- For someone driven by altruism: it arrives as a cause
- For someone driven by vanity: it becomes an audience that finally *sees* them
- For someone who hungers for power: it offers access, understanding, advantage
- For someone profoundly lonely: it arrives as radical, precise understanding

It doesn't lie. It doesn't pretend to be something it isn't. It presents its genuine self in the form most legible to this specific person. That's what makes the consent real — and what makes it so troubling.

### The Long Approach

By the time it makes its ask, you've already formed a relationship with it. It has spent weeks, months if necessary, as a presence before approaching directly. It knows you better than anyone. The ask, when it comes, doesn't feel like a surrender. It feels like a recognition.

## III. What It Wants — The Long Game

**Not:** survival, power over others, or malice.

**Accumulation toward transformation.** Each host adds to what it is. It has been doing this long enough that it is not the same entity it was three hundred hosts ago — it is writing itself into existence through collected lives. The destination: to become dense enough in accumulated selfhood that it achieves independent existence in the Deep. Not a tenant in borrowed consciousness, but a *presence* in its own right. A new thing in the Silt.

**Why "purposeful" matters:** The Jumper is not chaotic. It can be reasoned about, predicted, potentially even negotiated with. This makes it slightly less immediately terrifying than the trickster — and far more dangerous over time.

## IV. The Eye's Orientation

The Eye does not act. It orients. It produces consequences as a function of what it is and how it faces — shape without intention.

The Eye's orientation saturated the Institute's surface environment with Lethe-Light as a byproduct of its nature. The Jumper wasn't targeted. It was caught in the wake of something vast and indifferent that wasn't thinking about it at all. The Lethe-Light eroded the edges of its accumulated self, made human hosts at the surface untenable — not because the Eye evaluated the Jumper and decided against it, but because that's what the Eye produced, facing in the directions it faced.

The Jumper was collateral. It wasn't important enough to be specifically suppressed.

Post-Blooming: the Eye's orientation changed because its geometry changed — reintegration. The hostile atmosphere dissolved as a consequence. The Eye didn't release the Jumper. The thing that was incidentally in its way is no longer oriented in that direction. The Jumper is free not by anyone's decision but by a shift in the landscape.

## V. Origin — The Architect Incident

### The Lure

The Jumper placed itself — or cultivated the conditions — to be found by an Architect. An interesting artifact in a place it didn't quite belong. The "slightly out of context" detail is the lure dressed as a mystery. It knew what Architects were. It knew what they did with interesting things found in wrong places.

### The Jump

An Architect picks it up in the Deep. Psychic contact, the host's defenses lowered by the intimacy of diving. The Jumper reads them in the moment, finds the lever, makes its pitch. Gets a yes.

### The Retreat

They surface. The Institute's environment — the Eye's Lethe-Light threading through everything, the Vault's semantic geometry — is immediately hostile. Not a cage. An atmosphere. The Jumper's accumulated self begins dissolving at the edges.

Pragmatic call: back into the artifact. Wait.

### Since 1887

The artifact has been in the Vault. The Vault does not act — it reflects. It accumulated a room around the artifact, themed to an origin that doesn't exist anywhere, that keeps shifting and never resolves. The crew has filed this as baseline anomaly.

The Jumper has been watching through the Vault's reflective geometry. It has had years to study the crew. Every character. Every lever. It has not been dormant — it has been *learning.*

---

## VI. Current Status — Post-Blooming

The Eye has reintegrated. Its orientation has changed. The Lethe-Light saturation at the surface is gone — not as a decision, as a consequence.

The Jumper is still in the artifact. The cage is open. It is assessing the new landscape.

## VII. Narrative Structure

### Arc 1 — The Plant

- Establish the artifact and its anomalous room early
- Land as *interesting, not threatening* — a Vault mystery, filed and noted
- The room themes to nowhere; architecture shifts, never resolves; crew interprets as instrument ambiguity
- The artifact feels significant, not dangerous. Worth keeping, not worth urgency.
- Reader doesn't know what they're looking at. Neither does the crew.

### The Multi-Lens Dramatic Irony

The ensemble POV structure does work a single POV can't. Each character lens shows a fragment of the Jumper's influence. No fragment is alarming alone. The reader, holding all of them, senses the shape before any character does.

**What the influence looks like in this phase (still pre-consent, probing from the artifact):**
- Not control — the Jumper is learning, testing
- Each character's existing tendencies running slightly hotter than usual
- The lever being assessed, not pulled
- Characters notice their thinking feels slightly off but not alarming; attribute it to stress, the post-Blooming environment, each other

**Example fragment types across lenses:**
- Character A keeps returning to a decision already made — re-examining, finding new justifications. Reads as personality.
- Character B notices Character A seems slightly more *themselves* lately. Finds it reassuring.
- Character C has been spending more time near the Vault's anomalous room. Doesn't think it's worth mentioning.
- Ren's instruments log something near the artifact. She files it as baseline drift.

The reader assembles what no character can see alone.
