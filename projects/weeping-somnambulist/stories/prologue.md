## Beat 1 — The Courtyard

The day they arrive.

Erzsébet is in the kitchen when she hears the gate. She comes out to the
courtyard expecting deliveries and finds two young women — one with a
folder of documents under her arm, already looking at the roofline,
already taking inventory. The other standing in the middle of the
courtyard doing nothing.

Just standing there. Looking at something that isn't there.

Erzsébet has lived on this campus her whole adult life. She knows what
the building does to people who are sensitive to it. She's watched it
happen — the pause, the intake of breath, the quality of attention going
somewhere else. She's seen it in practitioners who came for Walpurgisnacht,
in certain children from the school, in Zoltán sometimes when he thinks
she's not watching.

She's never seen it happen this fast. The girl is still holding her bag.
She hasn't put it down yet.

The one with the documents introduces herself. Erzsébet listens and
watches the other one over her shoulder, still in the middle of the
courtyard, and thinks: *this one is going to change things.*

She goes to put the kettle on.

---

## Beat 2 — Walpurgisnacht, the Elevator

First Walpurgisnacht with the new occupants.

Erzsébet doesn't go down to the basement. She never has. That's not her
place and she's always known it without needing to be told. She stays
upstairs, keeps things warm, makes sure there's food when people come
back up looking like they've been somewhere difficult.

But she's at the elevator when the group assembles. She watches them
load in — the old ones who've been coming for years, the new faces, the
serious man with the tattoos at his wrists who Zoltán says has been
attending longer than any of them.

And Nia, near the front, saying she's especially excited. The ley line
storm, the confluence, all of it coming together tonight. Saying it the
way you'd say you're looking forward to a good rain.

Erzsébet watches the tattooed man look at her sideways. Watches someone
else's face go briefly pale. Watches Nia, who is not performing anything,
who means exactly what she said, who finds this genuinely delightful.

Dóra is in the back of the elevator. Erzsébet catches her daughter's
eye and reads her face: *which one do I believe?*

She knows which one Dóra is going to choose. She's already chosen it
herself.

The elevator descends. Erzsébet goes back to the kitchen.

---

## Beat 3 — The School

A Tuesday afternoon in spring. The second year.

Erzsébet is crossing the corridor outside the music room when she stops.

The school director found her in February, worried — one of the regular
teachers had been displaced by the Mordaunt woman taking over a Tuesday
slot, something about ear training, creative listening, and would Erzsébet
perhaps have a word. Erzsébet had said she'd look into it.

She'd forgotten. Or put it off. She's been putting it off for two months
because the children have not complained and the parents have not
complained and the school director is the only one with a problem and the
school director's problems are not always problems.

Now she stops outside the door and listens.

Nia is not playing anything. She's not explaining anything. She's just
sitting with twelve children in a circle and the room is — 

Erzsébet doesn't have a word for it. The room is *attending*. The
children are sitting the way children almost never sit, not rigid,
not performing stillness, just — present. Somewhere that is both here
and not quite here.

One of them is her youngest. Lili, seven years old, sitting with her
eyes half closed and a small private smile, listening to something
Erzsébet cannot hear.

She stands in the corridor for a long time.

She doesn't have a word with the school director.

---

## Beat 4 — Tuesday Afternoon (Final)

The last one. Erzsébet doesn't know it's the last one.

She brings tea to the music room afterward, the way she's started doing.
Nia stays late sometimes, tidying, airing the room out. They've fallen
into the habit of twenty minutes together before Nia goes back down.

Today Nia is at the window looking at the courtyard. The garden is coming
in — the beds Nia talked Erzsébet into expanding last autumn, the things
they planted together, starting to show.

Erzsébet pours the tea and doesn't say anything for a moment.

"The children were good today," she says finally.

"They're always good," Nia says. Not modestly. Just as a fact. "They
already know how to listen. I'm just reminding them."

Erzsébet thinks about Lili humming in the kitchen last week, something
without a name, something she'd learned in that room. She thinks about
the way the building felt the day these two arrived. She thinks about the
woman downstairs with her documents and her operation and her careful
not-looking.

She wants to ask something. She's not sure what.

Nia turns from the window. She looks at Erzsébet with the full quality
of her attention — the thing that stops people, that made the building
change the day she walked into the courtyard — and smiles.

"Thank you for the tea," she says.

Erzsébet goes back to the kitchen.

She doesn't see Nia again after Thursday.

*Cut to present. Six years later.*
