# Horror Night — Lorcan and Ama

## Lorcan

He watches for four weeks before he joins. This is not hesitation —
it's reconnaissance. He attends the movie, observes the methodology,
maps each person's tells. IO builds traps: textbook, readable, research
too on-the-nose. Nia becomes the environment: the space itself goes
wrong, which is not something you can defend against, which is why the
rule exists — don't give Nia a foothold. Ren plays easy prey and then
doesn't.

He understands what this is. Cubs wrestle. It's how they learn what
they can do. Horror night is training disguised as play, the same way
every meaningful game is training disguised as play. He approves. He
says nothing.

On the fifth week he joins without announcing it.

He doesn't build a trap. He doesn't become the environment. He does
one thing: he makes it real.

Not a figure, not a mechanism, not an aesthetic. The specific quality
of something that has actually been in the Deep's deep places and knows
what lives there and is now standing in your dream space with all of
that knowledge active in its posture. For approximately thirty seconds
the game stops being a game.

Then he releases it. Completely, cleanly, the way you'd set something
down.

The crew surfaces. Nobody speaks for a moment.

He says: *"That's what it actually feels like. Now you know."*

IO files it as calibration data. Ren files it under a category she
hadn't had before. Nia is quiet in a way that is different from her
usual quiet — not shaken, but respectful, the way you're quiet near
something genuinely large.

He does it once a season. Never the same shape twice. Just often enough
that everyone knows the real thing is possible, and they carry that
knowledge into every other horror night, and into every dive.

This is the instruction. He just doesn't call it that.

## Ama — The Park Bench

IO sets the stage beautifully. She always does.

A park on a warm afternoon. The specific quality of summer light through
leaves. A bench in a clearing, positioned just so, with the kind of
compelling rightness that makes you want to sit. Ama sits. The trap
is designed to make her lie back, stare at the clouds, let the afternoon
take her — and then spring.

Ama lies back. She enjoys it. IO sets a great stage.

The storm moves in the way IO designed it — fast, the sky going green,
parents gathering children, people running for cars. The trap springs.
Ama can't move.

The storm bears down. The first fat drops. Then the rain, serious and
cold. Thunder close enough to feel.

Ama closes her eyes and smiles contentedly, letting the rain wash her.

A sound in the distance. Low, continuous, building. Not thunder.

Not a train either.

Her ears pop. She sits up.

The tornado is moving across the park toward her, the specific roar of
something that doesn't have opinions about what's in its path. The trap
has her. The bench has her. She cannot move.

She closes her eyes again.

IO appears beside her, looking at her with genuine curiosity. *"You're
supposed to be frozen with fear."*

Ama opens her eyes. She considers the tornado. She shrugs.

She reaches into her jacket and hands IO a paper bag. Surprisingly dry.

*"Open it,"* she says.

IO opens it. Inside: a small purple drawstring bag. She recognizes it
immediately — the specific weight of it, the particular purple, something
she hasn't seen in years, something she would have said was gone. She
opens the drawstring. Stops. Her face does nothing, which means
everything.

*"Where did you find this,"* she says. Not a question. A very controlled
not-a-question.

Ama smiles.

---

The others arrive the way they do at the end of a horror night — drawn
by the resolution, wanting to see who scared whom. Ren, Nia, Lorcan,
materializing at the edge of the clearing while the tornado continues
its indifferent path in the background.

Ren asks what's in the bag.

*"You all have bags,"* Ama says.

They look down. Each of them is holding a brown paper bag they weren't
holding a moment ago.

Ama says: *"Go on."*

---

**Lorcan** opens his with the care of someone who has handled significant
things. He looks at what's inside. His poker face almost cracks —
something moves across it, fast, contained. After a long moment he looks
at Ama and cocks his head.

She meets his eyes and nods once. Barely perceptible.

He closes the bag carefully. Sets it down with the deliberateness of
someone handling something that matters. Looks out at the storm, already
passing — the tornado gone, the sky clearing the way dream skies do,
immediate and total.

He doesn't say what was in it. Nobody asks. That's the tell — that
nobody asks. Lorcan, who has been in the Deep's deep places, who holds
the warden contract, who does not rattle — closed the bag carefully and
won't speak. The crew registers this without discussing it.

---

**Nia** opens hers last. Her face does something private and complete.
She closes the bag gently, the way you'd close a book you've just
finished, and looks at Ama with an expression that is not surprise —
more like recognition. Like being seen by someone who sees correctly.

*"How did you know?"* she says.

*"I didn't,"* Ama says. *"It was already there. I just didn't look away."*

---

**Ren** opens hers with methodical care — not hesitation, procedure.
She looks at what's inside for a long moment. Then she laughs. Short,
genuine, slightly helpless. The laugh of someone shown something so
precisely accurate it bypasses every defense.

She looks up at Ama.

*"This is a primary source,"* she says.

Ama waits.

*"I've been trying to locate this for three years. I had it filed as
unrecoverable."*

She looks back into the bag. The laugh is gone now, replaced by
something quieter and more complicated — the specific feeling of finding
something you'd given up on in a place you never thought to look.

She closes the bag with both hands. Holds it in her lap.

*"How,"* she says. Not quite a question. The beginning of one she hasn't
finished forming.

*"You kept circling the same place in your work,"* Ama says. *"Always
approaching, never arriving. Whatever is in that bag is what you were
circling."*

*"You read that from my dreams."*

*"I read it from your attention. Dreams just make attention visible."*

Ren is quiet for a moment.

Then she opens her notebook.

---

**IO** is still holding hers. She hasn't opened it fully — got it
halfway and stopped. She's looking at what's inside without taking it
out. The others can't see what it is. Her face is doing nothing.

She closes the bag.

She doesn't say where Ama found it either.

Later, alone, she opens it again. Looks at it for a long time. Puts it
somewhere she doesn't usually put things — not filed, not archived, not
organized into the operation. Just somewhere. A drawer. The small purple
drawstring bag with its specific weight, its particular color, something
she would have said was gone.

She doesn't take it out again for a long time.

She doesn't throw it away.

---

*What was in each bag is never stated. The bags are whatever each
person most needed to find and had stopped believing they could.
The reader assembles their own version from the character's response.
What matters is not the contents — it's that Ama found them without
being told where to look.*

*She read it from their attention. Dreams just make attention visible.*
