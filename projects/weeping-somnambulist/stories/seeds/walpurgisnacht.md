# Walpurgisnacht

Early period. First Walpurgisnacht with IO and Nia in residence. The
gathering has been held at the Institute for as long as anyone attending
can remember — longer than the building. The Architects formalized it.
The Kovács family has prepared for it every April 30th for four
generations. This year the new occupants didn't know it was coming.

Lorcan arrives because he always arrives. Budapest is a primary node.
He's been attending this gathering since before the Institute existed.
The Kovács family knows him by name.

---

More people follow. Practitioners from across the network — some who
remember the Architects, some who don't, all of them with opinions about
the new occupants and the property and what it means that Mordaunt women
are holding it now. They arrive and there is food and the social layer
of a community that only sees itself a few times a year. Nia is
immediately at home. IO is doing rapid inventory.

At some point the question of who is going downstairs arises. In the
Architect years, this was managed — selected people, controlled access,
the basement treated as a sanctum with a velvet rope.

Nia doesn't do velvet ropes.

Anyone who wants to go down can go down. You should know it can get
weird. Everyone who's been doing this for more than a season already
knows that. The ones who are new find out tonight.

Dóra appears at the edge of the group. She's been on this campus her
whole life. She found the east wing passage at eleven and kept it between
herself and her father. She has been waiting for an invitation that was
never going to come through official channels, so she asks. Out loud. In
front of everyone.

Nia says yes before she finishes the question.

IO looks at Zoltán. Zoltán gives a small nod. She's ready. That's that.

---

The elevator is old. Cage style, slow. It holds them closer together
than comfortable.

Nia is near the front. She's been vibrating with something since they
decided to go down — not nerves, the opposite. She says she's especially
excited this year. Major confluence event. The ley line storm building
on top of Walpurgisnacht, the boundary already thin, all of it coming
together tonight.

She says it the way you'd say you're excited for a big storm rolling in.
The kind that lights up the sky. The kind worth watching from the porch.

Lorcan gives her a side stare.

Not alarm. Recalibration. He's been in Budapest for three weeks because
the gradient shifted — quality change, not just volume, something in the
texture of the Itch he couldn't name. He's been treating tonight with
the attention a major ley line storm on Walpurgisnacht deserves. He's a
professional. He checked his equipment. He's not going to do anything
stupid.

Nia is treating the storm like it's the whole point.

Which means either she doesn't understand what she's standing next to —
and she should, she should be reading this as serious — or she has a
relationship to the storm that's categorically different from everyone
else in this elevator. He's not sure which possibility is more
significant.

Across from him, someone's eyes have gone wide. A practitioner who
works smaller nodes, confluences where a major ley line surge is
theoretical rather than a regular fact of life. They came for
Walpurgisnacht. They came for the gathering, the thin boundary, the
work. They did not come for this. Budapest on Walpurgisnacht is already
serious. Budapest on Walpurgisnacht during a major ley line storm is a
field in a thunderstorm.

Nobody says anything for a moment.

The elevator descends.

Dóra is in the back. She doesn't know what a ley line storm means yet.
She doesn't have the vocabulary. But she's spent her whole life reading
this building's moods and she knows how to read a room.

She looks at Lorcan's face. She looks at the wide eyes across from him.
She looks at Nia.

She decides which one she believes.
