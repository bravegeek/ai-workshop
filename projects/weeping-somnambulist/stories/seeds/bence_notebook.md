# Bence's Notebook

He starts it when he's fourteen. Not because anyone told him to — because
the building does things he can't explain and he has his father's instinct
for not explaining things before he understands them.

The notebook is meticulous in the ways that matter. Dates, times,
locations, weather, what he observed, what he did not observe but inferred,
and a strict column on the right margin for things he cannot yet categorize.
The right margin column is often the longest. He doesn't find this
frustrating. He finds it honest.

What he records:

- Barnaby's position in the building at various hours, cross-referenced
  with events in the basement. Barnaby moves before things happen. The
  correlation is not perfect but it is not random.

- The east wing passage. He found it at eleven, told his father, they
  agreed not to mention it. At fourteen he starts documenting when it's
  open and when it isn't. The pattern resists his theories. He keeps
  looking for a better theory.

- Pressure changes in the basement corridor before and after dives.
  He doesn't know what dives are. He knows the corridor does something
  different on certain nights and he can feel it in his ears, like
  altitude change.

- The Hum. He can hear it sometimes, faintly, near the cistern door.
  He describes it as "a frequency below hearing that you feel in your
  back teeth." He does not know what the Hum is. He has recorded it
  forty-seven times by the time Ren reads the notebook.

- Nia's Tuesday class. He was in it for two years. He records what he
  noticed during the sessions — not what Nia taught, which resists
  description, but what happened to the other children. The stillness.
  The specific quality of attention. His own experience he marks as
  "compromised data — cannot observe self objectively." He was thirteen.

What the notebook doesn't have:

Framework. He doesn't know about the Psychosphere, the Eye, ley lines,
practitioners. He has the observations without the vocabulary. This is
not a flaw — it means his data is uncorrupted by the categories that
shape what experienced practitioners think to look for.

---

**Ren reads it in Arc 2.**

She asks Bence, who is twenty-two now, if she can see the notebook.
He hands it over without hesitation. He's been waiting for someone to
ask.

She reads it in one sitting. She comes back the next day with three
questions. He answers all of them. She comes back again.

What he recorded about Barnaby's positioning, cross-referenced with
her own instrument logs from the same periods, produces a correlation
she hasn't seen before. She doesn't tell him immediately what it means.
She asks him what he thinks it means.

He thinks about it for two days and comes back with an answer that is
not quite right but is aimed at the correct thing. She tells him where
his reasoning goes wrong. He adjusts. They go again.

This is how it starts. Not recruitment — recognition. He has been doing
the work for eight years without knowing that's what it was.
