# The Caerulite Reveal

First Walpurgisnacht. The group has descended. The cistern room holds
them closer than the upper floors did — the low ceiling, the heptagonal
pool, the smell of mineral water and something older underneath.

The practitioners who have been coming here for years notice it first.
The formations around the cistern rim — blue-white crystals, larger
than anyone has seen at a working site, clustered thick at the waterline
and spreading up the stone in branching structures. Not residue. Growth.
Whatever the Budapest confluence produces, it has been producing it here
undisturbed for decades.

Someone asks why they haven't harvested it yet.

IO says they haven't had much time to tidy up.

She means it as a deflection. The room receives it as confirmation: the
new occupants don't know what they have. The person who asked is already
doing math.

Then Nia says she took a big piece and made some art with it.

She crosses to the far wall and flips a switch. Ambient light suffuses
the room — blue, soft, the specific quality of light that seems to come
from inside the stone rather than from a source. Several people go still.
Practitioners who have worked with cistern residue scraped from mediocre
sites, diluted and impure, are looking at a formation that represents
years of their supply in a single room.

Nia watches them for a moment. Then she presses a second switch.

UV light hits the installation.

The Caerulite doesn't glow. It radiates — the distinction matters, the
way the difference between a candle and a flare matters. The crowd's
faces are lit from below, blue-white, the shadows running the wrong
direction. Someone inhales sharply. Someone else takes a step back not
from fear but from the instinctive response to proximity to something
more concentrated than expected.

Nia is watching people react to her art. She is quietly delighted.

Lorcan is not watching the art. He is watching IO.

IO is watching the crowd. She has the cognitive science background to
connect what she's seeing — the compound, the reaction, the Architects'
journal descriptions she's been reading — into a single coherent picture.
She is doing it now, in real time, in front of a room full of people who
already know what she's just figuring out.

She knows what the Institute is sitting on.

She knows the room knows.

She doesn't look at whoever asked about harvesting. She doesn't need to.
She can feel the quality of their attention from across the room — the
specific focus of someone who came here tonight having done their
research and is now confirming what they suspected.

Lorcan says nothing. He has seen what Caerulite does to a crew over
time. He watched it do it once before, in this building, to people who
thought they understood what they were working with. He watches IO's
face complete its calculation and files what he sees there.

Later, when the crowd has come back upstairs and Erzsébet has put out
food and the social layer of the gathering has resumed, IO finds Nia
in the corridor.

"You knew what it was," IO says. Not an accusation. A question.

Nia considers this. "I knew it was beautiful."

IO looks at her. "That's not the same thing."

"No," Nia agrees. "But it's where I started."

---

*The question the scene leaves open: who asked about harvesting? They
came to this Walpurgisnacht knowing the Budapest site had a history.
They scanned the cistern immediately. They will be back.*

*This is also the moment IO begins to understand what the Institute
requires protecting — not from the outside world, but from the
practitioner community itself. The operation's protective logic starts
here, before the operation has a name.*
