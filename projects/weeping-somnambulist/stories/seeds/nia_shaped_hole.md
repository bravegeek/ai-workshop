# The Nia-Shaped Hole

She made the neighborhood warm. When she left, the warmth didn't vanish
all at once — it drained slowly, the way heat leaves a room after the
fire goes out. People noticed without knowing what they were noticing.

---

**The school, three streets over:**

The school director remembers the Tuesday class. Parents still mention
it occasionally — their children were different afterward, more present
somehow, more attentive. Several of the children from those years have
developed unusual sensitivity to atmosphere, to the emotional temperature
of rooms, to things slightly off in ways they can't name. None of them
know why. None of them know to connect it to a Tuesday afternoon and a
woman who called it "creative listening."

The youngest ones don't remember her clearly. They remember a feeling.
Lili Kovács, nine years old in Arc 1, hums something without a name
when she's doing something else — washing dishes, walking to school.
She doesn't know where it came from. It came from those Tuesdays. It
came from somewhere Nia went and brought back.

---

**The Kovács family:**

Erzsébet doesn't talk about it. She lights a candle on the date. She
keeps Nia's teacup — the one Nia always reached for — at the back of
the cabinet. Not as a shrine. Just: she can't put it with the others.

Zoltán notices things the building does differently. He noticed them
before too but there was always a possible explanation. Now he stands
in the main corridor sometimes and the building feels like a held breath
and he doesn't know what it's waiting for. He doesn't mention this
to anyone.

Dóra came home from university the week after. She didn't explain why.
She stayed for a month, fixed things around the building, went back.
She still comes home more often than she did before.

Bence added a section to his notebook. He labeled it: *Changed
conditions — cause unknown. Baseline re-establishing.* His right margin
column for that period is entirely full.

---

**The practitioner community:**

The Walpurgisnacht gathering the year after is quieter. People who
met Nia once — in the elevator, in the upper rooms, in the brief warm
social layer of the gathering — feel the absence without knowing quite
how to account for it. She was there for one Walpurgisnacht. One. The
impression shouldn't have been that strong.

It was that strong.

Lorcan doesn't say anything at the gathering. He stands near the
elevator for a while before going down. He goes down.

---

**IO:**

She doesn't stop. That's the thing people don't understand — how she
keeps going, how the operation keeps building, how she moves through
the world as though weight is just another variable to engineer around.

What they don't see: every room in the building has Nia in it. Not
as a presence — as an absence with specific dimensions. The courtyard
garden Nia talked Erzsébet into expanding. The east-facing window Nia
always stood at. The basement stairwell where Nia was always first.
The Tuesday slot in the week that is now just Tuesday.

IO moves through these spaces every day. She doesn't look at them
directly. She builds around them.

The knot in her chest that loosened the day they arrived in the
courtyard is back. It's different now. Tighter and more specific.
It has the exact shape of someone she's not looking at.

---

**What the neighborhood carries:**

A quality in the air near the Institute on certain evenings. Not
supernatural — just the specific texture of a place where something
significant happened and the place hasn't finished processing it.
Old cities carry their histories in their walls. Budapest more than
most. This block, this building, this courtyard: something came here
and loved it completely and followed something further than it could
come back from.

The city doesn't know this. The city knows the block the way it knows
a hundred other blocks — by texture, by feel, by the specific character
of the light in the late afternoon. It knows something is different
here without knowing what.

That's enough. Cities remember what they need to remember.
