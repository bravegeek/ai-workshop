# The Letter

IO turns 25 and a letter from a Vienna law firm barely finds her after
grad school. It takes three forwarding addresses. She reads it twice,
then calls Nia.

She's going to explain everything — the trust, the property, the address
in Budapest, what the Mordaunt family apparently held all this time
through a firm that sends quarterly correspondence to no one in
particular. She has the whole thing organized in her head before Nia
picks up.

Nia picks up and says: "Have you been to Budapest?"

IO stops. She's been doing this her whole life — watching her little
sister be one step ahead through means she can't account for — and she's
normalized it. She accepts it the way you accept weather.

"You got the letter too?"

"What letter. I want some magyar cake."

A pause.

"I'll book the train," IO says.

---

They arrive with IO's documents and Nia's sense of direction. IO has the
trust paperwork, the property records, the correspondence from the firm.
She's already thinking about the legal situation with the school upstairs,
what the building actually represents, what it means to hold something the
family lost.

Nia steps into the courtyard and goes quiet.

Not the cake. Not the building. Not anything she could name.

Just: *oh. Here.*

IO watches her and doesn't ask. She's learned not to ask when Nia goes
somewhere she can't follow. She looks back down at her documents and
gives Nia the moment.

Zoltán appears from somewhere in the building. He takes in the two of
them — IO with her folder, Nia still standing in the middle of the
courtyard looking at something that isn't there — and something in his
face settles. His family has been here four generations. He's been
expecting someone with that name to show up eventually.

He takes them inside.

IO catalogues. Room count, condition, what the trust actually covers,
where the school fits into the legal picture. Her mind is already
organizing what she's inherited.

Nia is somewhere else entirely.

She finds the stairs to the basement before IO finishes the first floor.
