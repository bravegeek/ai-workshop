# The Gift Bags

The gathering is winding down. People drifting back upstairs, the social
layer reasserting itself over the chapel layer. Nia says she'll stay
and tidy up a bit.

IO has a half-second of *she never tidies up* and then Madame Vásárhelyi
asks her something about the Institute's lease structure and by the time
IO's answered it, Nia is already gone.

---

Dóra is sixteen and still awake at midnight because nobody told her not
to be, and she followed Nia back down because Nia looked like she was
doing something. She was right.

Nia is at the cistern formation with her shoes off, moving carefully
along the shelf, breaking off pieces with the specific attention of
someone selecting rather than taking. She looks up when Dóra appears in
the doorway and waves her in as though she expected her.

*"Do you know where your mother keeps the small bags? Like — gift bag
size. Little ones."*

Dóra absolutely knows where her mother keeps the small bags.

She comes back with a box of them — white linen, drawstring, the ones
Erzsébet uses for lavender sachets. Nia looks at them and says
*"perfect"* with the conviction of someone who has never once questioned
her own aesthetic judgment.

They work together at the shelf. Nia selects each piece with care —
not the largest, not the most striking, but the ones that are doing
something. Dóra doesn't know what they're doing. She can feel that
they're doing it. She ties the drawstrings while Nia holds each bag
and listens to it for a moment before setting it aside.

*"What is this?"* Dóra asks.

*"A mineral compound,"* Nia says. *"Very old. Formed at a place where
certain — energies, you could say — run together for a very long time."*

*"Is it valuable?"*

Nia considers this with genuine interest, as though it hadn't occurred
to her. *"Yes, I think so. To the right people."*

*"And you're giving it away."*

*"I'm giving it to the right people,"* Nia says. *"That's different."*

Dóra ties another drawstring. The bag hums faintly against her fingers
and she decides not to mention it.

Before they go up, Nia sets one more bag aside and holds it out to Dóra.
She doesn't explain. She says: *"Open it somewhere quiet."*

---

Upstairs, IO is watching the door to the stairs and has been for ten
minutes when Nia appears with a wooden tray and sixteen small white
bags and the expression she wears when she's already decided something
and is simply waiting for the world to catch up.

*"Help me give these out."*

IO picks up the nearest bag. Feels the hum — small, clear, continuous,
the specific register of something alive in the way minerals get alive
after enough time in the right conditions. She puts it down. Picks it
up again. Does the math that you do when you know what caerulite trades
for and there are sixteen bags on a tray.

She looks at Nia.

Nia looks back at her with the serenity of someone standing on a bridge
who has already jumped.

IO picks up the tray.

*"Where did you get gift bags?"*

*"Erzsébet's lavender supply. I'll replace them."*

They move through the room together. Nia carries half, IO carries the
rest. It takes IO approximately three exchanges to understand that Nia
is not presenting these as anything. She's pressing each one into
someone's hands with warmth and a specific sentence — something she
noticed about each person during the evening, something she remembered,
something that makes the recipient feel seen — and moving on.

No announcement. No ceremony. Just: *it was so lovely to meet you,
please take this small thing.*

The room goes quiet in the particular way that rooms go quiet when
something unexpected is happening and no one wants to be the person
who breaks it by reacting wrong.

Nia reaches Madame Vásárhelyi and says something about her knowledge
of the western confluences that makes the old woman straighten almost
imperceptibly. The bag exchanges hands.

She means it, IO thinks. She's not performing. She actually thinks
these people are wonderful and wants them to know it and happens also
to have a tray of caerulite.

---

Lorcan is near the window. He's been near the window for the last
fifteen minutes, which is how IO knows he's watching without wanting
to look like he's watching. He received his bag — IO saw Nia give it
to him with something that might have been a wink — and he is holding
it in one hand with the careful stillness of someone who knows exactly
what it is.

When IO passes him on her last circuit he looks at her.

The look says: *one hundred and fifty years in this world and I have
never seen anyone do this.*

Not: she just gave away a fortune. He's past that. More like: she
walked into a room full of people who have spent their lives accumulating
advantage and leverage and knowledge as protection — who have had to —
and she simply didn't. And the room doesn't know what to do with that.
The room has no framework for it. The room is going to be thinking
about this for a long time.

IO looks back at him.

Her expression says: *I know. I had half a second. She never tidies up.*

He almost smiles.

---

On the way downstairs afterward, Dóra finds IO in the stairwell.

*"Was it very valuable?"* she asks. *"What we gave out."*

IO considers her for a moment.

*"Each bag could have funded the school for a year."*

Dóra absorbs this. Then: *"She knew that, didn't she."*

*"Most likely,"* IO says. The way a parent says it about a kid who
jumped in every puddle on the way home from the bus stop. Of course
she knew. She also genuinely wanted to. Those two things were never
in conflict for Nia.

*"She set one aside for me too,"* Dóra says. *"Before we came up."*

IO stops on the landing.

Dóra is already heading down.
