# Weeping Somnambulist: Narrative Principles

## The Lens Principle

The story is told through multiple POVs. The lens shifts in pursuit of good storytelling — not on a rotation, not to ensure each character gets equal time. The lens is a tool. It shifts when a different perspective is the most powerful way to deliver a specific moment.

The question is never *whose chapter is this* — it's *whose experience of this moment is the one the story needs.*

---

## The Characters as Lenses

### IO
The operational present. Precise, controlled, unreliable in specific ways the reader learns to read. She converts grief into missions — which means her POV renders everything as purposeful when it isn't. She doesn't look at things directly. The past breaks through despite her, not because she allows it.

Use IO's lens when: precision is the point, when the gap between what she reports and what's happening is the story, when the reader needs to feel the architecture she's built around a wound.

Avoid IO's lens when: the story needs to see her clearly. She can't narrate her own face.

### Nia
The most powerful lens available, and the most constrained — she's gone by Arc 1. Her chapters are prequel and flashback, but placed where the story earns them, not where backstory is owed.

What Nia's POV gives that no other lens can: the thinning from the inside, experienced as deepening rather than loss. The reader in Nia's head sees the tragedy forming before IO does. Her sections establish what full-presence actually feels like — which makes her absence in Arc 1 felt rather than explained.

Use Nia's lens when: the story needs to hold both truths at once — IO's grief and Nia's peace — and only direct access can do it.

### Ren
The witness. She was in the original circle, slightly outside the IO/Nia bond, less distorted by grief and differently limited. She source-attributes everything; she freezes when data doesn't fit the framework; she is the most rational voice in a room that defeats rationality.

Her POV is the cleaner account of the early period. Her lens on her own Eye-connection revelation — trying to source-attribute her best work and failing — is the only way to deliver that scene fully. Post-reintegration her sections may carry something that isn't entirely hers: a frequency she can feel the difference of but can't name.

Use Ren's lens when: reliable witness is needed, when the story needs to see IO from outside, when the analytical framework hitting its limit *is* the story.

### Ama
The permeable one. Her POV is the best vehicle for the Deep from the inside — not navigating it but being received by it. She experiences others' emotional states as physical intrusion. Her discomfort about the extraction economy is ecological: she notices what the operation costs the Deep before anyone frames it as a cost.

Use Ama's lens when: the Deep's interiority matters, when the moral weight of the operation needs to be felt rather than argued, when belonging is the operative word.

### Lorcan
The boundary. His POV is about edges — what he holds, what he contains, what he watches himself not becoming. He's sitting on compressed knowledge about IO's operation, chosen not to push on it. His warden tradition reads the Deep in terms of health and damage the others don't have language for.

Use Lorcan's lens when: the story needs the weight of what someone knows and won't say, when the boundary between one thing and another is the drama, when the compartments fail.

### Barnaby
Not an interior lens — a behavioral one. No interiority access. Close third that stays outside him entirely: where he moves, where he won't go, how long he holds still, what his attention is doing. The reader has exactly the access to Barnaby that Barnaby has to the crew.

This is the right register for him throughout: his intelligence and grief are legible through behavior, not stated. The prose mirrors the relationship — watching carefully from outside, inferring from what's observed. The reader learns the grammar of reading him in the same moment they meet him.

Use Barnaby's register when: his knowledge needs to be present without being explained, when the warning is visible before anyone receives it, when what he can't say is the point.

## Backstory as Earned Arrival

There is no prequel-as-block, no flashback-on-schedule. The backstory of IO and Nia — the early period, the thinning, the original circle, the morning Nia left — arrives when the present-tense story creates the pressure that makes it necessary.

The lens choice determines the delivery mechanism, not the other way around:
- A moment that needs Nia's inside view is a Nia chapter, placed wherever in the narrative the story needs it
- A moment that needs the witness account of something IO can't narrate is a Ren chapter
- The backstory is *earned* by the present-tense scene that requires it

**The morning Nia left** — probably Ren's lens. IO's version of this moment would be defended beyond legibility. Ren woke up to the absence. She watched IO's face when she understood. She can give the witness account of the thing IO can't narrate.

**The thinning** — Nia's lens. From IO's side it's a horror story of not recognizing what she's watching. From Nia's side it's deepening. Both are true. You need both lenses to hold both truths.

**IO's first deliberate extraction** — IO's lens, but what she allows herself to know is the question. The reader may see more than she admits.

---

## What the Multi-Lens Structure Builds

Each character is blind in specific ways. The full picture — of IO, of Nia, of what the operation is and what it costs — only assembles across all of them. No single POV has it.

The reintegration decision arrives at different times for different characters from different directions:
- Ren assembles the argument (containment playbook is a known failure)
- Ama feels it through what the Deep's ecology is doing
- Lorcan's warden instinct reads the wound as not healing
- IO has to be backed into it by all of them, and still has to make the choice alone

The multi-lens structure makes that convergence possible — and makes it land.
