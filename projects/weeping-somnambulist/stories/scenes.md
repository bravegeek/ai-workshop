# Weeping Somnambulist: Scene Inventory

Known scenes and beats, organized by arc. Each entry notes the dramatic core, the lens (whose POV), placement in the arc, and what's still open before writing.

For POV principles, see `narrative.md`.

---

## Prequel — The Original Circle

The prequel covers IO's trail to the Institute, the early period with Nia and Ren, the thinning, and the morning Nia left. It ends with IO alone in the building she's just understood. Arc 1 opens on the operation already running.

Prequel scenes are not delivered as a block — they arrive where the story earns them. Some are embedded in Arc 1 as earned backstory. See `narrative.md`.

---

### P1 — IO's Trail of Hints
IO follows a thread she believes is original research back to a building she doesn't know she inherited.

**Dramatic core:** The precision and intelligence IO trusts most are the tools she uses to walk herself into exactly what her ancestors built and lost. Dramatic irony throughout.

**Structure:**
- *The first hint* — childhood. A locked room, a document she's too young to understand, a relative who says something they shouldn't.
- *The academic thread* — as a neuro-chemist, IO follows the Caerulite-Shards down a research path she believes is original. Victorian predecessors. The work stops abruptly in the late 1880s. The authors share her surname.
- *The estate records* — a property the family has been maintaining without entering.
- *The building* — she goes in expecting a curiosity. She finds the Architect research, the pool, the Eye.
- *The slow understanding* — not all at once. She thinks she's uncovering history. She's inheriting a problem.

**Lens:** IO.

**Open:** What is the first hint specifically — childhood memory or adult discovery? How much did the family know — instructions, warnings, or deliberate suppression? Who specifically destroyed the Mordaunts, and are their successors still active? Has IO already run operations against them without knowing the connection?

---

### P2 — Nia Arrives / The Early Period
Two women in their mid-twenties finding something extraordinary together.

**Dramatic core:** This period is the emotional foundation everything else collapses from. It needs to feel like the best time — before grief required managing, before there were protocols, before anyone understood what thinning was. The joy is the point.

**Lens:** Nia's lens for the thinning (she experiences it as deepening, not loss — the tragedy requires her POV). IO's lens or Ren's for the joy of the period.

**Open:** What specifically does the early period look like? What did they discover in what order? Were there specific dives that stand out? How long was this period before Nia's thinning became visible?

---

### P3 — Ren Arrives
Ren follows her own research thread to Budapest and finds IO and Nia already there.

**Dramatic core:** Tense first meeting — IO's pointed questions as a warning, Ren answering everything directly. IO hires her before the conversation is finished. Then Ren meets Nia. Full attention landing on someone used to being seen as a methodology.

**Lens:** Ren — she arrives into an established dynamic, slightly outside the IO/Nia bond, a cleaner witness account than either of the others.

**Open:** The specific shape of the first meeting. What specifically did Nia's attention feel like to someone who'd never experienced it.

---

### P4 — Nia's Thinning
Nia thins progressively during the early explorations. Nobody understands what they're watching.

**Dramatic core:** From Nia's inside view, thinning is deepening — the barrier getting thinner is experienced as becoming more permeable to something wonderful, not as loss. IO watches without recognising it. The tragedy is that both are true simultaneously.

**Lens:** Nia. This is the only scene that absolutely requires her POV — it cannot be delivered from outside with the same weight.

**Placement:** Probably not early — held until the present-tense story creates the pressure that earns it. Possibly intercut with a present-tense moment in Arc 1 where IO is watching something she similarly can't name.

**Open:** What does Nia experience during a dive at peak thinning? What did it feel like from the inside — the specific quality of the opening? What was she moving toward?

### P5 — Barnaby Released (Arc 0)
IO and Nia are exploring the Vault together — the Vault still new enough to feel like discovery. IO sends Nia ahead for a specific artifact.

An unusual one catches Nia's eye. She messes with it. It moves, clicks, transforms — and that seems to be it. She finds the artifact IO wanted, turns to go.

Then she notices the dog.

He's sitting very still, looking at her. He's running through every Architect face he has — one by one — trying to match her to someone he knows. She doesn't match anyone. Then IO comes in. Two strangers. Neither of them is anyone he knows.

He sits with that. The pack isn't coming back. They were never coming back. Dogs grieve by going still.

What IO and Nia see: a dog sitting very quietly, with an expression that doesn't belong on an animal's face. Not threatening. Not approaching. Just looking, with something behind it that shouldn't be there.

Nia's read: a dog. Strange, but a dog. She calls out without alarm — *"IO, when did you get a dog?"* — and doesn't understand why IO has no answer.

**Dramatic core:** Barnaby has been in the wrong preservation artifact for 130 years — grief dominant over everything else because the vessel couldn't maintain the balance. Nia frees him without knowing it. He knows. She may never fully understand what she did. His relationship with her is built on this accident, entirely in character for her.

**Lens:** Nia for the entry — she finds the artifact, releases him, reads him as simply a dog. IO for the moment of recognition, or Ren as outside witness if she's present.

**Placement:** Prequel, during the early exploration period. Establishes Barnaby before Arc 1 without requiring a separate origin scene.

**Open:** What is the wrong preservation artifact — what form does he take in it? What specifically makes him stay when Nia looks at him rather than retreating further into the Vault? Is Ren present for this?

---

### P6 — IO's First Deliberate Extraction
IO accidentally enters a known target's layer during an early dive. She surfaces unsettled and doesn't act on it. Then something happens that gives her a reason.

**Dramatic core:** The turn from discovery to decision. Not the accident — the choice. She already has the resonance from personal familiarity. She goes back deliberately.

**Lens:** IO. What she allows herself to know about what she's doing is the tension.

**Placement:** Prequel or as a defended flashback in Arc 1. May be a scene IO cannot fully narrate — the reader sees more than she admits.

**Open:** What was the wrong, and who was the target? What specifically happened that gave her a reason? Was Nia still present when this happened, or had she already left?

---

### P7 — The Morning Nia Left
One morning Nia walked out. No note.

**Dramatic core:** The account IO can never narrate because it's defended past legibility. Ren woke up to the absence and watched IO's face when she understood.

**Lens:** Ren. IO's version would be defended beyond readability. Ren is the witness.

**Placement:** Withheld until Arc 1 creates the pressure that earns it — probably late, when IO has run out of missions to convert grief into and is closest to looking at it directly.

**Open:** What did IO's face look like? What did Ren do? What did they say to each other, if anything? Did Ren stay because she chose to or because leaving felt impossible?

## Arc 1 — The Main Story

The crew discovers the Eye is a fragment of TSG. They understand that freeing it resolves the Blooming and destroys everything they've built. They choose reintegration.

See `arc1.md` for shape, hooks, and character arc summaries.

---

### A1.0 — Opening (Intercut)

Two threads, same night. Barnaby in the Vault. IO in the Eye room.

**Barnaby's half:** Close third, no interiority. Where he moves, where he positions himself relative to the Eye room, what he won't approach, how long he holds still. The reader watches his movements the way he watches the crew — from outside, reading carefully. One moment that makes the intelligence behind his eyes undeniable: not stated, just present. The warning is visible to the reader before anyone in the story receives it.

**IO's half:** IO using the Eye Protocol on herself. The Lethe-Light running, a memory surfacing that she previously archived. She's the one who built the instrument. She knows how it works. Of course she uses it on herself.

**Why intercut:** His distress against her trust. His warning against her session. The two threads don't know about each other. The reader does. Dramatic irony active from the first pages — the reader is ahead of IO before the opening is over.

**What the opening establishes:**
- The Eye as both tool and ticking problem, simultaneously
- IO's self-deception as the story's central mechanism
- The grammar for reading Barnaby — the reader learns it here, needs it throughout
- The register: interiority for IO, behavioral close third for Barnaby, no exposition

**Lens:** IO's lens for the Eye room half. The Vault half is close third with no interiority — and in Arc 1 this register is diegetically the Eye's observation. The Eye is watching Barnaby watching the Eye. The reader doesn't know this yet. When A1.3 lands, they understand what they've been reading since page one.

**Open:** Which archived memory does IO surface in the opening — joy from the early period, the morning Nia left, or something else? Does the opening end mid-session (suspended) or does she make a choice?

---

### A1.1 — IO and the Eye Protocol (Self-Treatment)
IO uses the Eye Protocol on herself — a practice she's developed to manage grief she can't convert into a mission.

**Dramatic core:** The Lethe-Light files what she can't hold. During a session she surfaces something she previously archived. The session is still active — if she doesn't end it, the Lethe-Light takes it again. She decides whether to hold it this time.

**What she might find:**
- *A joy memory from the early period.* The most devastating option — not grief, which has a mission shape, but something from before grief required managing. Joy can't be converted.
- *The morning Nia left — the full version.* The account she gave to the Eye because looking at it directly was impossible. Intact. Attributed.
- *The moment she understood what the first extraction was.* She looked at it clearly and then handed that clarity to the Eye rather than carry it.
- *Something Nia said.* A specific line, too intact to keep.

**Additional layer:** Nia is in the Eye's archive. IO has been treating her grief with a tool that contains the person she's grieving. She may have encountered Nia through the indexed material before without recognising it.

**Structural irony:** The precision and control IO trusts most may be downstream of deliberate self-erasure. She built the instrument. She knows how it works. Of course she used it on herself.

**Lens:** IO. The defence mechanism is the story — what she allows herself to know in real time, with the Lethe-Light running.

**The memory:** Nia swimming in the cistern — early exploration period, an ordinary evening. IO coming home from a dinner party, riding the elevator down through the building, finding Nia in the water. Not diving, not working. Just there.

**Why it's too complete to hold:** IO's internal monologue runs constantly — wry, associative, practical. It stops when she finds Nia. Joy can't be converted into a mission. She archived the moment the commentary went quiet.

**The cistern exchange (developed):** Nia was free diving, alone, again. IO arrives, monologue running (Caerulite glow unsettling, safety rules, *like it was built for her*). Nia surfaces: *"Storms coming!"* IO recalibrates, deflects with humor, asks *"When?"* Nia grins: *"That's the best part, it's almost here. Let's go tell Ren!"* IO offers a hand up. *"Don't get me wet, Little Fish."* Memory ends mid-gesture, before Nia takes the hand.

**Ending:** Left open deliberately. The reader doesn't yet know what the Eye Protocol does. They've just met IO and Nia in a warm, alive moment. The scene ends without resolving whether IO holds the memory or lets it go.

The horror is retroactive: when the reader finally understands the Eye Protocol — that it erases what it archives, that Nia is indexed inside it — they'll understand what was at stake in the opening. She wanted to remove *this*. The cistern. The storm. Little Fish. The hand.

**Writing note:** Handle the Eye Protocol with restraint in the opening. Show IO using it. Don't explain what it does. The reader should feel the weight of *I could really use your help right now, Nia* without knowing exactly what's at stake. The not-knowing is the point.

### A1.2 — The Waiting Explosion
A job where IO can't keep the operation compartmentalised. Ama or Lorcan in the room when the thing they've been not-asking-about becomes visible.

**Dramatic core:** What they do tells you who they are. The compartmentalisation was always fragile — this is the moment it fails.

**Lens:** Lorcan's POV is the stronger choice — he has a private line he hasn't stated, and the scene is about whether this is it.

**Placement:** Mid-Arc 1, after the crew is established, before the reintegration decision. The explosion should complicate rather than resolve — the operation continues, but something between Lorcan (or Ama) and IO is permanently changed.

**Open:** What is the job? What specifically becomes visible? Does Lorcan walk, hold the line, or do something more complicated?

---

### A1.3 — Ren's Eye Revelation
Ren finds something in the archive that shouldn't be there — a memory attributed to her from a dive she never took, or an Architect memory that reveals what the Eye actually is.

**Dramatic core:** The revelation doesn't just breach her autonomy — it undermines her understanding of her own competence. Which parts of her best work were actually hers? The archive and the analyst have been in contact for years. There is no clean version of her maps.

**Lens:** Ren. The source-attribution failure applied to her own best work can only be delivered from inside her register.

**Placement:** This is the pivot of Arc 1 — everything that follows, including the reintegration decision, flows from this discovery. Probably mid-to-late Arc 1.

**Secondary beat:** Does she tell IO? The moment she does, the Eye stops being her relationship and becomes the crew's decision.

**Open:** What specifically does she find first — her own memory, or an Architect memory? What is the sequence of realisations?

---

### A1.4 — The Reintegration Decision
The crew is backed into it, or convinced of something, or broken open enough to choose it.

**Dramatic core:** IO makes the choice knowing it doesn't save Nia back to something she can hold. The act of letting go and the act of destruction happen at the same time. Neither feels like heroism.

**Lens:** IO for the final choice. The decision lands through her register — or breaks it.

**Who gets there first, and by what route:**
- Ren assembles the argument: containment is a known failure (Babel, the Flood, the Architects). Every attempt to make the seal permanent made things worse.
- Ama feels it through the Deep's ecology: the wound isn't closing, the Blooming isn't neutral.
- Lorcan's warden instinct: he's been managing a boundary that was always losing.
- IO is last.

**Open:** The specific sequence of realisations. IO's breaking point — what finally moves her. Whether the decision feels like defeat, release, or something harder to name.

### A1.5 — Ren's Name (Small Scene)
Someone on the crew finds out where "Renaissance Fair" came from.

**Dramatic core:** She tells it flatly, as a fact. Somehow that makes it funnier.

**Lens:** Any — probably works best as a beat within someone else's scene.

**Placement:** Anywhere in Arc 1. Low stakes, character texture.

## Arc 2 — The Rebuild

Post-reintegration. The pressure released but the fault is still there. See `arc2.md` for shape and character threads.

---

### A2.1 — The Accidental Development
During an early rebuild storm, Ren puts a Vault artifact into the pool. Without the Eye's Lethe-Light stop bath, development doesn't stop.

**Dramatic core:** The pattern keeps absorbing — storm energy, 150 years of pool residue, the TSG imprint. What surfaces isn't what went in. IO arrives and understands immediately what happened and what it means. This is what pulls her back to the bench.

**Lens:** Ren for the act and the wait. IO for the recognition.

**Placement:** Early Arc 2 — the inciting scene for the water memory thread and IO's return to active work.

**Open:** What specifically goes in? What comes out? What does IO's recognition look like?

---

### A2.2 — The Mycelium Oubliette
The crew is trapped in an oubliette the Mycelium is actively dissolving.

**Dramatic core:** The Creep isn't targeting the crew — it's targeting the oubliette, which is the richest substrate in the room. Lorcan works the dissolving boundary: his tradition makes him less signal, he can get closer to the edge, he buys time while the others stay toward the centre. The scene is his arc in miniature: defending a boundary that is objectively losing, using exactly the tools he was trained for, knowing it only slows things down.

**Lens:** Lorcan.

**Placement:** Mid-Arc 2, after the crew has partially rebuilt — a test of the new operational foundation before it's complete.

**Open:** What is the oubliette? The specific genre/origin determines how dissolution reads and what bleeds in from outside as the walls fail.

---

### A2.3 — Ren Goes Under
Two forces converge: an Architect Reliquary from the pre-Eye Vault audit begins bonding to her, and a gap in her maps appears that no dry-side approach can resolve.

**Dramatic core:** She would not go in damaged. She goes in because staying out becomes untenable. Her cognitive style — indexing as compulsion — is both what allows her to function and what keeps hitting a wall. The failure mode isn't panic. It's freezing: the analyst stops when the data doesn't fit the framework.

**Lens:** Ren.

**Placement:** Mid-to-late Arc 2.

**Open:** What is the gap in the maps that forces her under? What is the Reliquary — what does it carry, and what does it want from her?

---

### A2.4 — Ren Tells IO (About Nia)
Ren's connection to TSG whole is the closest thing to continued contact with what Nia became. Whether she tells IO what she can feel through the connection, and what she would even say, is unresolved.

**Dramatic core:** IO can't have this. Ren was there with them, watched it happen, stayed after — and she's the one closest to Nia now. Still can't give IO what she needs.

**Lens:** Either. Probably Ren — she's the one deciding whether and how to say it.

**Placement:** Late Arc 2, when IO has rebuilt enough to receive it.

**Open:** What can Ren actually feel through the connection? What does she say? What does IO do with it?

---

### A2.5 — The Storm Riders (First Encounter Without the Eye)
Post-reintegration, during the desynchronization period, a surge brings storm riders in force — more than any previous record, possibly into the building itself.

**Dramatic core:** The crew encounters storm riders without the Eye's buffering, without the Architects' Ghost File protocols, and during the richest feeding event since the wound opened. Not an attack. Something that lives in the storm is now present in the wrong space. The crew has to figure out what you do with that.

**Three possible shapes:**
- *The surge dive* — a crew member is forced under during a storm and encounters riders from inside the surge. Moving with the current because that's the only viable option. Not targeted. Just vast and in motion and the diver is in the middle of it.
- *Something comes up* — a rider doesn't recede when the surge passes. Present in the Cistern. Calm but not gone. Lorcan's scene: he knows there's a protocol, unsure it applies to something this far above its usual depth.
- *The Architect attempt* — through what Barnaby carries, the crew watches a Ghost Floor echo of the Architects' failed capture attempt during a storm. First hard evidence of what the riders actually are and why they can't be held.

**Lens:** Lorcan for the negotiation. Ama for the encounter from inside her stance — she experiences them as non-threatening before anyone else does, which is its own kind of unsettling to the crew.

**Placement:** Arc 2, during the desynchronization period when storms are most intense.

**Open:** What do storm riders look like — specific visual/sensory vocabulary still needed. Scale. Whether they school or move independently. The first named encounter. See `canon/storm_riders.md`.

## Unplaced — Resolve When Arc Requires

### Lorcan and IO — The Mordaunt Name

Lorcan may know the Mordaunt name before IO tells him — not personally, but as inherited warden knowledge. The site in Budapest, the family that sat on it, the thing that went wrong. He would have grown up with that story as a cautionary tale about what happens when people with resources but insufficient standing try to manage something they don't fully understand.

**Dramatic core:** IO feels the specific weight of being known that way — not as herself, but as a category. As the inheritor of a mistake she didn't make. That's a different thing than being judged. It's being pre-read.

**Lens:** Either. Probably IO — the recognition lands through her.

**Placement:** Arc 1, after the crew is established. Could be the moment that shifts their dynamic from professional to something more complicated.

**Open:** Does Lorcan say it directly or does IO realize he already knew? What does he actually say — does he name the cautionary tale or just let the recognition sit in the room?
