# Weeping Somnambulist: The Biology of Diving

## I. The Inheritance Problem

Diving changes the body. Those changes persist.

Sustained exposure to the Psychosphere — through Caerulite, through Lethe-Light proximity, through repeated contact with the Deep — causes epigenetic modifications. Not changes to DNA sequence. Changes to gene expression: methylation patterns, histone modifications, the regulatory architecture that determines which genes activate and when. These modifications are produced by environment. They are also heritable.

A family that dives across generations accumulates these marks. The Mordaunt attunement is not only practice building on practice. It is biological inheritance of diving-adjacent states across generations: each generation's exposure leaves marks the next generation carries before they have ever dived.

---

## II. What the Modifications Affect

**Boundary sensitivity.** The epigenetic marks associated with Caerulite thinning reduce the expression of whatever maintains self-edge integrity at the cellular level. Children of heavily thinned divers may be born with thinner boundaries — more permeable to the Deep, more sensitive to gradient, but with less margin before permeability becomes structural damage. The Mordaunt lineage has been accumulating this particular modification for at least four generations.

**Caerulite response.** Long exposure reshapes how the body processes the compound. Inherited sensitivity means Mordaunt-lineage divers respond to smaller quantities, reach depth with less apparent effort, but also have a compressed window before dose becomes thinning. What registers as a functional working dose for a first-generation diver may already be past the threshold for an inherited-sensitivity diver who doesn't know their own baseline.

**Experience integration.** The Deep generates experiences faster than they can be consolidated. Regular exposure appears to produce heritable changes in how the brain processes and integrates ambiguous, multivalent, or temporally disordered experience — a faster processing speed in certain kinds of perceptual overload, combined with a higher baseline of unresolved material. Both are adaptive under diving conditions. Neither is neutral in waking life.

---

## III. The Serotiny Angle

Some epigenetic expressions are suppressed under normal conditions and require specific environmental triggers to activate. Bloom conditions — the elevated gradient of an active Blooming — may function as one such trigger, unlocking dormant expressions in diver lineages that cannot switch on under normal substrate pressure.

The crew may be demonstrating capacities during this Blooming that they cannot access outside it. They have no way of knowing which abilities are permanent and which are serotinous — which will persist after the Blooming resolves and which will go quiet when the gradient drops. The question will only be answered after reintegration, when the crew discovers what they've lost.

---

## IV. The Ancestor Connection

The Ancestor Sleeper's zones amplify what is already present in epigenetic inheritance. A visitor with no diving lineage encounters diffuse ancestral weight — general, difficult to read. A diver whose family has worked the Deep across generations carries epigenetic modifications from that exposure. In an Ancestor zone, those modifications are the channel.

The Sleeper's presence does not give access to collective human memory. It gives access to the specific line — the predecessors who did exactly this work, in exactly this substrate, surfacing as body knowledge in a descendant who has never met them. Not narrative memory. The specific emotional register of a Mordaunt ancestor navigating a structure IO is about to enter. The physical knowledge of a depth they both reached. The déjà vu belongs to someone else.

For IO: she carries at minimum four generations of epigenetic diving marks in a lineage that was already biologically close to the Deep before the Mordaunts made it a trade. An Ancestor zone would be uniquely legible to her, and uniquely disorienting. She would not be able to distinguish her own instincts from inherited ones. Whether those instincts are more reliable than hers is a question with no clean answer.

---

## V. The Architect Echo

The crew is running the same experiment the Architects ran, one generation compressed into one lifetime.

The Architects accumulated epigenetic exposure over decades of active work before the dissolution began. The crew has better safety protocols. They do not have better understanding of what their exposure is doing at the cellular level. The thinning they experience as structural damage to self-edge may be the same epigenetic modifications the Architects accumulated — observable only when they have progressed past the point of correction.

No member of the crew has examined their own epigenome. None of them know whether the changes they are accumulating are transmissible. Ren, as the non-diver, is the control — her epigenome carries Lethe-Light exposure and sustained Eye proximity without the Caerulite and diving load. Whether that constitutes a different kind of exposure, or protection, or neither, has not been established.

The crew is the Architects' echo: same substrate, same proximity, safety protocols that work without anyone understanding the full mechanism of what they're protecting against.
