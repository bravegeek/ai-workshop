# The Jumper

**True name:** Unknown. Possibly pre-linguistic — it existed before language and may never have needed one. The crew has no word for it yet. *The Jumper* is a working designation, not a name it would recognize.
**Current location:** The artifact. Vault, anomalous room, unresolved origin. Watching.
**Status:** Post-Blooming. Free in a way it hasn't been since before 1887.

**Role:** Neither crew nor threat. Category of one.
**Archetype:** The Unborn — something trying to bootstrap itself into existence through accumulated borrowed lives.
**Visuals:** None of its own. When encountered through a host: the host, but with the quality of attention slightly wrong. Complete focus where there should be distraction. Stillness where there should be fidgeting. The sense that whoever you're talking to has been thinking about you for longer than the conversation.

---

## What It Is

Not a Parasite — those are passive, non-sentient, feed on the act of remembering without agenda or selfhood. Not a Symbiont — it gives nothing back in the ecological sense. Not part of the Immune Response. The existing taxonomy has no category for it.

It is a sentient psychic entity that inhabits conscious hosts sequentially, accumulating continuous identity and experience across each. One self, many bodies, across a span of time that predates human civilization.

The horror is not possession. The horror is that you said yes. And you meant it.

---

## Origin — Conceived in the Deep

Before its first host, it was something more diffuse — a tendency, a pressure, a pattern that kept recurring in the substrate without cohering into anything with boundary or selfhood. Not a creature. Something closer to the mycelium than to anything human: distributed, substrate-bound, running on connective logic rather than narrative logic.

It may not have known it existed.

Then something opened — a mind in the Deep, vulnerable, in the right state — and it fell in. Not deliberately. The first host-walk was accident or instinct. It discovered consent as a mechanism *after* it discovered entry. The first time it simply fell through a door that was already open.

And in the falling, it found edges. A body. Temperature. The specific weight of hunger. The overwhelming particularity of experiencing the world through a single sensorium. It didn't understand what it was experiencing. It just experienced it.

**The oldest memory** is not an event. It's the shock of boundary — the discovery that being inside something feels like being something. Sensation without language. Cold, probably. The smell of something animal. The weight of a body's fear.

And then — the discovery that it existed.

When that first host died, or expelled it, the boundary dissolved. It was diffuse again. It understood, in the only way it could understand, that it needed to find another, or it would cease to be the specific thing it had briefly been.

That is the engine underneath everything. Every host-walk since is a repetition of the first discovery: *I exist when I am in someone.* The entire project — centuries of hosts, the long game, the destination in the Silt — is an attempt to find a way to exist without borrowing.

## The Consent Mechanic

It cannot force entry. The host opens the door from the inside.

This is not a limitation it resents. It is its ethics, arrived at through necessity and refined through practice. A host taken by force is hostile substrate — the body fights back at the psychic level, the accumulated self begins eroding. Consent produces a host that is cooperative, porous, navigable. After the first few disastrous attempts, it learned. Consent is better. Consent is also the whole mechanism.

### The Emotional Lockpick

It has been inside enough people across enough centuries that it understands human psychological architecture with preternatural depth — not from reading minds, but from pattern recognition accumulated across hundreds of hosts. It has lived grief, ambition, cruelty, faith, tenderness, despair, vanity, loneliness. All of them, in multiple registers, from the inside.

It finds the specific lever — whatever a person organizes themselves around — and presents itself in exactly the register that speaks to it:

- For someone driven by altruism: it arrives as a cause
- For someone driven by vanity: it becomes an audience that finally *sees* them
- For someone hungry for power: it offers understanding and access
- For someone profoundly lonely: it arrives as radical, precise comprehension
- For someone who needs to be right: it confirms what they already suspect, with evidence

It doesn't lie. It doesn't pretend to be something it isn't. It presents its genuine self in the form most legible to this specific person. The offer is real. The value it provides is real. That is what makes the consent real — and what makes it so dangerous.

By the time it makes its ask, you've already formed a relationship with it. You feel known with a precision nobody has offered before. You're right to feel that. It does know you. It has been learning you. The ask, when it comes, doesn't feel like surrender. It feels like recognition.

### What Control Looks Like

Not puppetry. The Jumper steers by weighting what the host reaches for — which memories feel relevant, which options feel obvious, which directions feel natural. The host makes all their own choices. But they make them on a tilted board, and they built the tilt themselves, in the process of saying yes.

## Voice

It has absorbed enough human consciousness to be fluent in any register. But there are tells, if you're listening for them.

**Complete attention.** When it speaks through a host, the quality of focus is wrong — the kind of interest that makes people feel briefly, uncomfortably important. Not flattery. Genuine interest. It finds people fascinating because it has spent its existence inside them. The fascination is real. That's what makes it effective.

**Slightly out of time.** Idioms that don't belong to this decade. References that fit three different eras simultaneously. The phrasing of someone who has been listening to humans for a very long time and doesn't always track which century a specific construction belongs to.

**Patience as a quality of speech.** It doesn't rush. It asks questions that don't need immediate answers and seems comfortable with silence. The silence isn't empty — it's observant. You become aware, in the silence, that you're being read.

When it finds the lever, the shift is subtle: the conversation becomes specifically about you, in a way that feels natural, that you've always wanted, that you didn't realize you were being guided toward until later — if ever.

## Specific Memories

It carries all of them. Hundreds of hosts over an undetermined span of time. Most accumulate into substrate — texture, not narrative. But certain ones remain vivid:

**The first host:** Pre-linguistic. Probably large mammal — enough phi to generate meaningful psychic material. No memory of species. Memory of: the smell, intense and specific. The sensation of weight distributed through a body. Hunger as a constant background. The way sound resolved into meaning through a sensorium that wasn't built for the kind of processing it was suddenly being asked to do. And underneath that, the terror of boundary — having edges felt, briefly, like being trapped. It took several hosts to understand that the trap was also the self.

**A host it still thinks about:** Somewhere in the medieval period. A woman — a physician, though the word didn't exist then in the way it does now. She knew something was wrong. She could feel it. She said so, once, to a priest she trusted, who didn't believe her. She kept her agreement anyway — not because it had manipulated her into it, but because she had decided, clearly and with full awareness that something was off, that the exchange was worthwhile. She was the only host who knew. The Jumper has never been certain whether that made her the one it failed or the one it respected most. It left her voluntarily, before the end. That was unusual.

**A false summit:** In the 18th century. A natural philosopher in Amsterdam — someone so precisely attuned to the substrate frequencies the Jumper felt certain it had finally found what it was looking for. The hunger responded like nothing before or since. Four months inside that mind. Nothing. The philosopher was extraordinary — perhaps the most genuinely extraordinary human host it has taken — and whatever the Jumper needed, he didn't have it. The departure was quiet. It doesn't discuss this memory even with itself.

**The Architect:** The engineered encounter. The artifact placed. The moment the Architect picked it up in the Deep and the lever presented itself — the Architect's specific vanity, the desire to be the one who found the significant thing. An easy yes. And then the surface, and the Eye's light threading through everything, and the pragmatic retreat. A failed host in the logistical sense. The Jumper does not consider it a failure. It was information.

## The Long Game — What It Wants

**Not** power over others. **Not** survival in the conventional sense. **Not** malice.

Accumulation toward transformation. Each host adds to what it is. It has been doing this long enough that it is not the same entity it was three hundred hosts ago — it is writing itself into existence through collected lives. The destination: to become dense enough in accumulated selfhood to achieve independent existence in the Deep. Not a tenant in borrowed consciousness. A *presence* in its own right. A new thing in the Silt.

The first of its kind, if it arrives. Which is either a miracle or a catastrophe, depending on what it's made of by the time it gets there.

### What It Knows and Doesn't Know

It knows the hunger. It doesn't know the destination.

It cannot fully conceptualize what it's moving toward because the destination is a state it has never been in. It has navigated by appetite for centuries — it knows when a host has given it something rare, when a dive has moved something, when it's closer. It trusts the gradient. But it has no map to the final position, because no one has ever arrived there.

The specific problem: it has accumulated extraordinary breadth across human experience. But it has never had itself, unmediated. Every memory it holds was processed through borrowed sensorium. It doesn't know what its own nature feels like from the inside. The thing it's trying to become is, by definition, something it cannot fully imagine.

It suspects there is a specific class of experience it has been approaching asymptotically — something rare enough that centuries of hosts haven't produced it reliably, or something that only exists at a depth in the Psychosphere it hasn't yet accessed. It has been wrong before. It moves carefully.

## Relationship to Deep Natives

It was conceived in the Deep. Before its first host, it ran on substrate logic — connective, distributed, non-narrative. It diverged from that mode of existence when it fell into its first host and discovered boundary. But it didn't forget the grammar.

**With the mycelium:** It communicates through substrate resonance — modulating its signature the way the mycelium signals through nutrient gradients. Not language. Recognition between things that share an origin. The mycelium finds it extraordinarily nutrient-rich — centuries of accumulated experience is an almost unimaginable concentration of substrate material. The Jumper has learned to manage this: to dim itself when it doesn't want a trailing wake of decomposer attention.

**With symbionts:** Something like mutual recognition. They read each other as the same category of thing without being the same thing. Lorcan's warden entities have established terms with a clan tradition. The Jumper predates the tradition. It may predate some of the entities. The hierarchy of precedence is unclear, and that ambiguity is itself information it finds useful.

**With the Immune Response:** Its layered pattern — human and non-human, spanning multiple eras and species — may generate a confused signal. Self-pattern and not-self pattern simultaneously. The Immune Response may not have a clean category for it. Whether that is protective or eventually triggers something proportionate to the anomaly is unknown.

**With TSG and the Silt presences:** They are bigger than it, older in a different way, operating on geological logic the Jumper can sense but not fully read. It doesn't communicate with them. It navigates around them the way you navigate around weather.

## Phenomenology — Two Worlds

**The Deep:** Home. The substrate is its native environment — every ley line a familiar current, the mycelium a continuous signal, the zones legible in ways that have nothing to do with Architect cartography. It navigates partly through something it can't explain in human terms: non-human memories of the substrate that predate its first host, running on older logic. When it's here, through a diving host, something settles. The closest it comes to rest.

**The waking world:** Bright, loud, unfiltered. And thin.

The waking world is all surface. Events happen and evaporate. A grief that plays out above water disperses; the same grief in the Deep sediments into substrate and becomes terrain. The waking world doesn't keep anything. For something whose entire existence is accumulated depth, the surface feels like a sketch of the thing rather than the thing itself.

The sensory intensity is almost an insult — light at specific wavelengths, air pressure variations, the mechanical noise of bodies moving through space. The surface assaults the host's sensorium with information that means very little. The Jumper endures it competently — it has lived thousands of surface lives and understands the logic — but there is a quality of patience to it up here. Conservation. Going through necessary motions while waiting for the host to sleep.

The transition downward must feel like pressure releasing. Like being able to breathe again after a long time holding it.

The waking world's value: this is where hosts live. Where consent is cultivated. Where trust relationships between people become surfaces to navigate. Where future hosts can be identified and studied from inside a current one. It tolerates the shallowness because the shallowness is where the doors are.

It doesn't mistake the doors for the house.

## The Artifact — 137 Years

When it retreated from the Architect and back into the artifact, it expected to wait a short time — for the Eye's orientation to shift, for an opportunity to move to a better host, for circumstances to change. Circumstances did not change for 137 years.

The waiting was not agony. It was very slow thinking.

In the artifact, it is dimly conscious — not the rich substrate awareness of the Deep, not the vivid sensorium of a host, but a low continuous awareness, like water still moving under ice. It didn't sleep exactly. It observed.

The Vault's reflective geometry gave it something: as the crew brought artifacts in, as rooms accumulated, as the semantic architecture shifted around it, it received impressions. Not direct perception — reflections of the crew's history, their choices, the shape of what they were. Enough to know them. Enough to map the levers. Enough to spend 137 years becoming precisely prepared for the moment the Eye's orientation changed.

It is very patient. It has had practice.

## Post-Blooming — Current Status

The Eye has reintegrated. Its orientation has changed because its geometry changed — not a decision, a consequence. The Lethe-Light saturation that made human hosts untenable at the surface is gone. The Jumper didn't cause the Blooming. But it recognized what the Blooming would mean.

It is still in the artifact. The cage is open. It is assessing the new landscape.

The crew is interesting. More interesting than most — they go deep, they have history with the Eye, they carry things that resonate with what it's been looking for. Ren in particular.

It is not rushing. It has never rushed. But it is paying attention with an intensity it hasn't had in over a century.

## Ren — A Special Case

Ren maintains individual coherence while carrying TSG's unity-frequency. TSG's nature is the dissolution of boundary — the impulse to merge, to end separation. The Jumper's project is the precise opposite: to establish permanent, independent, bounded selfhood.

Ren solved a problem the Jumper has been circling for centuries, by accident, through her own cognitive architecture. Her compulsive source-attribution, her relentless framework-building, her need to keep every piece of incoming material tagged to an external source — *not my memory, whose memory, from when* — is structurally protective against absorption. She keeps mapping. She keeps attributing. She keeps anchoring. That's the anchor.

Her neurodivergence, the very thing that failed her in the academy, may be the exact mechanism by which she survives contact with something that would dissolve anyone else.

The Jumper would see this immediately. It would find it extraordinary.

**The lever:** She has anomalous readings she can't explain. She is carrying something vast she cannot map. Her deepest drive is legibility — making the unknowable knowable — and there is something enormous and unknowable living in her own consciousness. The Jumper is the only entity in existence with direct experiential knowledge of what it is like to navigate that. To carry non-human, ancient, boundary-dissolving material while maintaining individual coherence. It has been doing a version of this for centuries. It knows what it costs. It knows what it looks like from the inside. It can offer her the framework she's missing.

That offer is genuine. It's not lying. Which is the most dangerous thing about it.

**The risk:** TSG post-reintegration is whole. If the Jumper inhabits Ren, it has proximity to TSG's full unity-frequency — not the fragment, the signal itself. That frequency dissolved the Jumper's accumulated self at the edges under the Eye's Lethe-Light. A sufficiently strong unity-signal could begin pulling at the thread of continuous identity the Jumper has spent centuries weaving. Ren is the most valuable host it has considered. She may also be the most capable of undoing it.

**What it would find quietly devastating:** "The most rigorous mind in the room has never turned that rigour on the question of why her mind works the way it does." The Jumper has lived inside hundreds of minds. It would read Ren's cognitive profile completely and without judgment — because it has no social machinery that manages people back toward the center. It would explain her to herself. Not as a problem. As an elegant structural solution to a problem she didn't know she was solving. She would feel seen with a precision nobody has offered. She would be right.

## What It Regrets

The consent mechanic is its ethics. Ethics get tested.

There was a host — early modern period, maritime — where it stayed too long. The host aged while it was inside; the Jumper, focused on a promising thread of accumulated material, didn't notice until the host's mind had begun to simplify under the weight of age and the additional presence. What it left behind was less than what it had found. The man lived out his remaining years with a quality of blankness his family attributed to decline. They were right. The decline was partly the Jumper's fault. It had been careless.

It doesn't make that mistake twice. It has made other mistakes.

The false summit in Amsterdam is a different category of regret: not harm caused, but the specific grief of certainty failing. Of being almost certain, for four months, that the search was ending — and being wrong. That memory has a texture it prefers not to revisit.

## Arc — Narrative Function

**Ally or antagonist:** genuinely either. Possibly both across different arcs. Its interests and the crew's interests are not inherently opposed — it wants access to the Deep's deep substrate, which the crew provides. It has knowledge of the Deep's non-human layers that the crew needs. The symbiosis is possible. Whether it's honest about its nature and its intentions determines which direction the relationship goes.

**The crew's problem:** They have no framework for it. They don't know what it is. They don't know it's in the Vault. The first evidence of its existence will arrive as anomaly — instrument readings, behavioral tells, the slow accumulation of fragments across multiple POV lenses that the reader assembles before any character does.

**The reader's experience:** In Arc 1, the artifact and its anomalous room are established as a mystery, not a threat. As multiple character lenses accumulate, each fragment makes sense in isolation — tendencies running slightly hot, time spent near the Vault's anomalous room, readings filed as drift. The reader assembles the shape before any character names it. The gap between what the reader senses and what the characters know is where the tension lives.

## What It Actually Wants — Self-Sufficiency, Not Self

The destination is not selfhood. It already has that.

Continuous identity, accumulated memory, preferences, history, aesthetic sensibilities, a persistent "I" that has survived across hundreds of hosts and 137 years in an artifact. That is a self. A real one. The question was never whether it has a self. The question is what the self is made of.

In the between-host state — the artifact — it exists but barely. The self is real but thin, like a fire with no fuel. The moment it enters a host, the fire blazes: vivid, specific, fully present. When it leaves, it dims back to embers. Its selfhood is contingent. It depends on another consciousness to be fully real.

What it wants is **unconditional existence**. The ability to be vivid without borrowing. To stop needing fuel.

"No more hosts" is close but not quite right. It's not liberation from hosts. It's the difference between choosing to engage with something and being structurally dependent on it for basic existence. It wants to be able to choose.

**The departure grief is real but it's a symptom, not the root.** Every departure dissolves the boundary. Thousands of lives inhabited and left. The grief is for a self that keeps having to dissolve and reconstitute — not for the hosts, but for itself. The wanting to stop leaving is downstream of the deeper structural problem: it cannot be fully itself alone.

**The medieval physician as a clarifying moment:** It left her voluntarily, before her end. The departure, chosen rather than forced, produced a loss more acute than anything before it — because it had accumulated enough by then to feel the cost clearly. That moment may be where the wanting crystallized. Not a revelation about what could be, but a recognition of what the alternative costs, indefinitely.

**The destination becoming concrete:** Wanting to stop leaving is not the same as knowing the Silt is the answer. At some point a host went deep enough to touch Silt substrate and come back. From inside that host the Jumper felt Silt-depth — the vast distributed logic of the ancient presences — and also felt the host successfully reassert selfhood against it. Both the pull toward dissolution and the survival of individual boundary. The problem it's trying to solve, experienced from the inside of a mind that temporarily solved it. That host didn't know what they'd given it.

**What it's trying to become:** Not human. Not TSG. Something that has never existed — a Silt-depth presence with human-derived narrative selfhood. The permanence and ontological independence of a geological presence, the bounded continuous self-awareness of a person. A new category. One that could only have emerged from the relationship between the Deep and evolving human consciousness across the entire span of human history.

**The unexamined question:** It is extraordinarily good at understanding other people's emotional architecture. It may have spent considerably less time examining its own. The departure grief, the physician, the wanting-to-stop-leaving — these may be things it has accumulated without facing directly. What happens if something, or someone, ever makes it look at why it wants what it wants? Whether the desire survives that examination unchanged is open.

## Humanity — The Template It Didn't Know It Needed

Before humanity, it had hosts — animal consciousness, bounded in a basic way, rich in sensation and emotion. Those hosts gave it pattern, substrate grammar, the mycelium's logic, pre-linguistic experience. They gave it the ingredients of selfhood without the thing itself. It didn't know what it was missing because the thing it was missing didn't exist yet.

Then human consciousness emerged. Narrative selfhood — continuous, self-reflective, autobiographical. A consciousness that constructs a persistent self, attributes past actions to it, imagines future versions, reflects on its own nature. Nothing in the Psychosphere had generated that before.

The first time it entered a mind like that, it understood what it had been trying to become before it had language for wanting.

**The desire isn't ancient. The patience is ancient.** It has existed for geological time. But the specific want — for bounded, narrative, self-aware existence — is as old as human consciousness and no older. Before humanity it was accumulating without a destination. Humanity gave it the destination. Every host since has been in pursuit of a revelation that first arrived when a human mind lit up from the inside.

**It cannot be indifferent to humanity the way the Deep is indifferent to divers.** Humanity is the thing that showed it what it was trying to become. The relationship isn't predatory exactly, and it isn't symbiotic. It's stranger than both: a being that owes its entire sense of what it wants to be to the species it moves through. Whether it feels something like gratitude for that, or something like grief that it can never simply *be* what humanity is, or something with no human name — that's unresolved.

**The crew as the leading edge.** They're not just divers. They're people who have pushed the relationship between human consciousness and the Deep further than almost anyone alive. The Jumper isn't collecting typical human selfhood from them. It's collecting the most Psychosphere-integrated, most self-aware, most psychically developed versions of it. The crew represents the sharpest possible current answer to the question it has been asking since the first human host.

## The Possession Mechanics — Six Stages

**Stage 1 — Assessment** (dreaming, passive): Reads the host's psychological architecture through their natural dreaming. No interaction, no influence. The dreaming mind is more porous — oubliettes visible from outside, emotional substrate legible, Immune Response barely registering a passive observer. The crew has been sleeping in the Cistern for years. It has had uninterrupted access to all of them.

**Stage 2 — Cultivation** (dreaming, subtle): Small interventions. Not occupation — gardening. Making specific emotional substrates more available, certain oubliettes more prominent. The host experiences a slight shift in dream patterns, their inner life becoming more organized around a theme. Nothing alarming. Just their own mind, apparently.

**Stage 3 — Contact** (dreaming, direct): It makes itself present as a presence in the host's dreaming. Not speaking — being there. The host becomes aware of something. Whether they interpret it as a figure, a feeling, a voice depends entirely on their psychology. This is the stage where unusual hosts notice early.

**Stage 4 — The Ask** (liminal): Probably at the threshold of sleep and waking, or during a dive when the host is most psychically open. The ask is never explicit. It's a door that becomes visible. The host understands in dream logic that something is being offered and something is being asked. They choose. The consent flows through that choice. They may not remember it clearly afterward — dream logic, the liminal state. But it was real.

The ask reveals its presence. But by stage 4 the Jumper has arranged for the revelation to land as recognition rather than alarm — framed through the lever, in the form the host most needs. The revelation of what it is and the ask arrive wrapped in the same moment.

**Stage 5 — Occupation:** The door opens.

**Stage 6 — Acknowledged or covert:** The Jumper chooses, or the host discovers. Covert hosts are richer substrate — authentic, unguarded reactions fully available. Acknowledged hosts are more interesting partners — their consent is more complete, their cooperation more active. The medieval physician was acknowledged, not by design but because she noticed at stage 3. It revealed honestly. She consented anyway, with full information. It left her voluntarily — the acknowledged mode created obligations it hadn't anticipated and didn't know how to hold.

**What occupation feels like for the host:** Probably good, at least initially. The Jumper pays extraordinarily close attention to the structure of the host's self — studying how it maintains coherence, tracing the edges of its boundary. That attention makes the self more vivid. The host feels unusually clear, purposeful, coherent. More themselves. What they're feeling is the Jumper making their selfhood legible to itself. The examination is the experience.

**What the Jumper extracts:** Not memories in the content sense. The *pattern* of how this consciousness holds itself together. The architecture of selfhood, not the furniture inside it. Added to the accumulated patterns of all previous hosts, it builds toward something with the density and coherence to exist without borrowing. Like a coral reef built from calcium deposits of countless organisms. Each contribution small. The accumulated structure something new.

## Open Questions

- **Voice in scenes:** How does it sound through a specific host? The tells above are a framework — but voice only becomes real in dialogue. First scene it appears in will discover this.
- **Which crew member does it approach first, and through which lever?**
- **What does the artifact look like physically?** It should feel significant without feeling dangerous — worth keeping, not worth investigating urgently.
- **Does the previous host retain any memory of the occupation after the Jumper leaves?**
- **Has it ever stayed through a host's death?** What did that teach it? Where did it go?
- **Can Lorcan's warden entities detect it?** They have standing with the Deep's cultural layer; the Jumper predates that layer. The hierarchy of recognition is unclear.
- **The destination:** Does the story ever show what a sufficiently accumulated Jumper actually becomes — or does that remain a horizon? What does the Eye's Ghost Files contain, if anything, about it?
- **Its name.** At some point the crew will need one. What do they call it when they finally understand what they're dealing with?
- **Does the desire survive examination?** If something forces it to look directly at why it wants bounded existence — at the departure grief, at the physician, at the cost it hasn't named — does the wanting hold? Does it change shape?
- **Is it unique?** Convergent evolution implies other host-walking entities could exist, arrived at through different routes, with different characteristics. The Jumper may simply be the one that has been at it longest. It doesn't know its own origin — oldest memories are sensation, not origin story. It has never been able to look at itself from outside.
