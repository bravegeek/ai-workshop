# Weeping Somnambulist: Artifacts

## I. What Artifacts Are

Artifacts are compressed Psychosphere material — pieces of experience that have become dense enough to solidify into object form. Each one is somebody's something: a fragment of extracted consciousness that retains the character of its origin. The Psychosphere doesn't keep facts; it keeps experiences. Artifacts are what experiences become when conditions are right for crystallization.

They have personality because they came from something that had character. They are not neutral tools.

---

## II. Where Artifacts Form

The Psychosphere is mostly Drift — open, undifferentiated flow of accumulated experience. Artifacts form where experience concentrates rather than flows.

### Oubliettes — Primary Source

An oubliette is a closed system: the same experience cycling through the same internal logic, building concentration indefinitely. Nothing escapes, nothing dilutes. The pressure produces crystallization. Artifacts form in oubliettes the way stalactites form in caves — the conditions are right for something solid to precipitate out of supersaturated medium.

Oubliettes are also organized. They have genre logic, internal rules, a character. The artifacts they produce reflect that organization. A Victorian oubliette produces Victorian artifacts. A collective grief oubliette produces grief-dense artifacts. The artifact is a product of its environment, compressed further.

**By depth:**
- *Shallow oubliettes* (upper zones) — personally scaled, formed from one person's intense recurring experience. Intimate artifacts. Self-sustaining; don't require a logic field to hold their shape at the surface.
- *Mid-zone oubliettes* — collectively scaled, formed from shared cultural or historical experience. Larger Semantic Volume. Require the Eye's logic field to surface intact.
- *Deep oubliettes* — ancient, possibly pre-linguistic. The Architects probably never fully entered these. The crew hasn't yet.

This is why extraction is professional work. Open Drift is accessible to anyone with basic ability. Oubliettes are where the good artifacts are, and oubliettes are dangerous — each one a sealed pocket with its own physics, its own genre logic, its own response to intruders. Navigating one without being consumed by it is the crew's core competency.

### Confluences — Secondary Source

Ley line intersections where different currents meet. The mixing creates pressure that can crystallize experience into object form. The Budapest confluence produces higher local artifact density than most sites, flavored by the site's specific layered history — Roman, Ottoman, Austro-Hungarian, wartime. Artifacts found here carry that sediment.

### Open Drift — Rare

Drift disperses rather than concentrates. Drift artifacts formed somewhere else — in a sealed pocket, at a confluence — and were swept into flow afterward. Like finding a gem in a riverbed: occasional, unpredictable, shaped by having been carried rather than sealed. Tend to be fragmentary, less internally coherent, more responsive to whoever finds them.

### The Silt — Unexplored

The deepest layer holds the oldest material. Pre-linguistic, possibly pre-human. The Architects never reached it. Nobody has. What artifacts exist here is unknown.

## III. The Three Eras

### Pre-Eye (Before 1887)

Without a logic field, only self-sustaining artifacts could be brought to the surface — things that hold their own shape without external support. These are personally scaled, emotionally dense, structurally simple. Folk-art strange.

Examples:
- *A specific skill at peak* — a virtuoso performance held in object form. Transfers temporarily to the user. The Architects used these extensively.
- *A recurring dream* — someone's specific recurring dream, extracted whole. Plays through when activated. Degrades over time; dreams don't keep well.
- *A lost language* — not just vocabulary, but the cognitive frame the language encoded. Using it changes how you think. Dangerous for extended use.
- *A childhood place* — a specific home or landscape from deep personal memory. Functions as a stable pocket in the Deep. Feels borrowed, because it is.
- *A grief* — a specific loss, extracted whole. Carrying it opens perception in ways that are useful and costly.
- *A mastery* — different from skill; a way of being in a practice, not a performance of it.

Barnaby knows which artifacts in the Vault are pre-Eye. This is the foundation IO and Ren need for arc 2 — artifacts that work at root level, without logic field support.

### During-Eye (1887–Present)

The logic field allowed extraction of things that couldn't survive the transition otherwise. Storm-Forging fixed their patterns using Lethe-Light as stop bath. Larger, more abstract, less personal.

Examples:
- *A collective fear* — not one person's, a cultural one. Extracted from depth. When activated, fills a space.
- *A historical event's emotional density* — not a replay, but the grief and terror of it compressed. Useful as a power source or a lens.
- *A forgotten god* — a deity whose worship has entirely ceased. Not a sleeping god — something smaller, genuinely gone except as echo. Residual properties from what it governed.
- *An architectural memory* — a building that no longer exists, intact. Not just the structure but the life that happened in it.
- *A pre-linguistic state* — Zone 4 material, barely objectified. The crew probably can't use these reliably. They sit in the Vault doing something nobody fully understands.

Some during-Eye artifacts are in field-dependent equilibrium — contents partially expanded by the Eye's field, stable only because the dissolution was continuous. Post-reintegration they revert. What they become is not always what the crew thought they were.

### Post-Eye (After Reintegration)

Uncharted. The direction is back toward pre-Eye character but with the crew's current understanding of what they're doing. Barnaby is the key to identifying which pre-Eye artifacts in the Vault remain viable models.

## IV. The Calling Mechanism

Artifacts call to certain divers through experiential resonance. An artifact formed from a specific kind of experience recognizes something matching in a diver — a quality, a wound, a question being carried. The recognition runs both ways: the right diver will feel a density in the flow before they see the artifact. Not a voice. A pull. A specific wrongness in the Drift that draws attention.

Different practitioners notice different artifacts:
- **Ama** — feels anything alive or relational. Belonging-dense artifacts surface toward her.
- **Lorcan** — notices things with strong, clear boundaries. Artifacts with defined identities recognize his Declare stance.
- **IO** — notices artifacts with concentrated force or power. May walk past intimate artifacts entirely without registering them.

The call is not always benign. An artifact might call because it recognizes a wound it can deepen, a quality it can feed, or a question the diver is carrying that the artifact contains the wrong answer to. The artifact's will determines whether the call is generous or predatory. Barnaby probably knows which ones are which.

## V. Extraction and Stance

An artifact that doesn't want to come creates different problems depending on how you respond:

- **IO (Impose)** — extracts against the artifact's will. It arrives, but damaged or hostile. She probably doesn't always notice the difference, or attributes it to other factors.
- **Ama (Belong)** — cannot extract against an artifact's will. What she can't persuade she leaves behind. What she brings back is more intact than what others carry, because she didn't damage it getting here.
- **Lorcan (Declare)** — negotiates directly. Works with things that respect direct address; fails entirely with things that don't have that kind of clear identity.
- **Barnaby** — inhabits rather than carries. Knows the Vault's long-term residents from the inside. His avoidance of specific artifacts is a judgment call based on information nobody else has.
- **Ren** — doesn't dive, but reads what comes back better than anyone. Probably has a private taxonomy of artifacts-that-arrived-wrong, cross-referenced with which diver extracted them and how.

## VI. Artifact Creation Methodology

A framework for developing specific artifacts:

**Step 1 — Find the source anchor**
History, myth, or pseudo-science that contains the experience. The source grounds the artifact in something real. Pseudo-science is useful specifically because it's often folk Psychosphere theory — an attempt to describe the Deep's mechanics in the language of the physical world. The artifact works in the Psychosphere not because the pseudo-science explains it, but because the pseudo-science was reaching toward something real.

**Step 2 — Identify what the Psychosphere kept**
Not the facts — the experience underneath the facts. The experiencer's view, not the observer's. What was it like from the inside? What quality of feeling was intense enough, and shared enough (or private enough), to leave a residue?

**Step 3 — Find the physical form**
The object embodies its content. Not arbitrarily — the form communicates what the artifact is before it's used. Something small and heavy. Something with moving parts that still work. Something ordinary that is wrong in one specific way you can't name.

**Step 4 — Determine what it does**
What does access to this compressed experience give the carrier? What's the cost? The cost is not a punishment — it's the terms of the exchange. What does this specific thing ask of the person who holds it?

**Step 5 — Give it a will**
What is it waiting for? What kind of person does it respond to, and what kind does it resist? What would it take for this artifact to give everything it has?

## VII. Crew Relationships to Artifacts

**IO** — instrumental. Artifacts are tools and products. She respects them the way a surgeon respects instruments — carefully, without sentimentality. She probably has private feelings about specific ones she won't admit to.

**Ama** — uneasy. She knows what artifacts are: extracted experience, things taken from where they belonged. The extraction economy sits badly with her even when she participates. She may have already returned one without telling IO.

**Lorcan** — develops relationships with long-term Vault residents. Probably talks to them. Notices when they change. Treats curation as wardenship: these things were somewhere, they're here now, that means something.

**Ren** — cataloguing curiosity. Knows every artifact's provenance and properties better than anyone, but externally. The Vault is her library. She has a private taxonomy IO doesn't know about.

**Barnaby** — the most intimate relationship. He's lived in most of them. He knows their characters from the inside. Some he likes; some he avoids; some he returns to. His preferences are information.
